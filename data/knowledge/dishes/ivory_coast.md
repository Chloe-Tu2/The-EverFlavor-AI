### Kedjenou

Kedjenou (also known as Kedjenou poulet and Kedjenou de Poulet) is a spicy stew that is slow-cooked in a sealed canari (terra-cotta pot) over fire or coals and prepared with chicken or guinea hen and vegetables.

- From: Ivory Coast
- Origin (Wikidata): Ivory Coast

Fun facts:

- (culture) Kedjenou is a traditional and popular dish of the cuisine of Côte d'Ivoire.
- (origin) Kedjenou comes from Ivory Coast.
- (culture) In Côte d'Ivoire, the dish is traditionally served with Attiéké, a side dish made with grated cassava.

### Acheke

Acheke (correct spelling being attiéké (Ivory Coast) or akyeke (Ghana)), is a dish made from cassava that is popular and traditional in the Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana.

- From: Ivory Coast
- Origin (Wikidata): Ivory Coast
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cassava root

Fun facts:

- (origin) Acheke comes from Ivory Coast.

### Cocoa bean

The cocoa bean, also known as cocoa () or cacao (), is the dried and fully fermented seed of Theobroma cacao, the cacao tree.

- From: Ivory Coast

Fun facts:

- (origin) The term cocoa beans originated in the 19th century; during the 18th century they were called chocolate nuts, cocoa nuts or just cocoa.
- (history) Chemical analysis of residue extracted from pottery excavated at an archaeological site at Puerto Escondido, in Honduras, indicates that cocoa products were first consumed there sometime between 1500 and 1400 BC.
- (about) The cacao plant was first given its botanical name by Swedish natural scientist Carl Linnaeus in his original classification of the plant kingdom, where he called it Theobroma ("food of the gods") cacao.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) The cacao tree was first domesticated at least 5,300 years ago by the Mayo-Chinchipe culture in South America before it was introduced in Mesoamerica.
- (origin) Cocoa bean is ultimately derived from kakaw(a), but whether that word originates in Nahuatl or a Mixe-Zoquean language is the subject of substantial linguistic debate.
- (origin) Cocoa bean was first domesticated at least 5,300 years ago, in equatorial South America at the Santa Ana-La Florida (SALF) site in what is present-day southeast Ecuador (Zamora-Chinchipe Province) by the Mayo-Chinchipe culture, before being introduced into Mesoamerica.
- (about) Evidence also indicates that, long before the flavor of the cacao seed (or bean) became popular, the sweet pulp of the chocolate fruit, used in making a fermented (5.34% alcohol) beverage, first drew attention to the plant in the Americas.
- (about) In 2024, global cocoa bean production reached 5.8 million tonnes, with Ivory Coast leading at 38% of the total, followed by Ghana and Indonesia.
- (about) At least 29% of global cocoa production was compliant with voluntary sustainability standards in 2016.
- (about) Cocoa contributes significantly to economies such as Nigeria's, and demand for cocoa products has grown at over 3% annually since 2008.
- (about) Not to be confused with cacao, Cocoa.
- (about) As of 2018, evidence suggests that cacao was first domesticated in equatorial South America, before being domesticated in Central America roughly 1,500 years later.
- (history) A Spanish soldier who was on Hernan Cortés' side during the conquest of the Aztec Empire tells that when Moctezuma II, emperor of the Aztecs, dined, he took no other beverage than chocolate, served in a golden goblet.
- (origin) Chocolate was introduced to Europe by the Spaniards, and became a popular beverage by the mid-17th century.
- (origin) Spaniards also introduced the cacao tree into the West Indies and the Philippines.
