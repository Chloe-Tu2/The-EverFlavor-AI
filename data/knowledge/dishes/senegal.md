### Chakery

Thiakry (also spelled thiacry or chakery, from Wolof: cakri), bodé in Fulani or dèguè in Bambara is a sweet millet couscous dish consumed in West Africa.

- From: Senegal

Fun facts:

- (culture) Its first recipe dates from the era of the Fulani people in present day northern Senegal who are traditionally herdsmen, and spread throughout West Africa.
- (about) The Gambian and Senegalese dessert is now known as a delightful afters in West Africa.The wheat or millet granules are mixed with milk, sweetened condensed milk, or yogurt, as well as dried fruit such as raisins, desiccated coconut, and spices such as nutmeg.

### Thieboudienne

- From: Senegal
- Origin (Wikidata): Senegal
- Heritage: intangible cultural heritage
- Ingredients (Wikidata): fish, rice, vegetable, Oryza molida, tomato sauce

- (about) In Pulaar it is known as maaro e liddi ('rice and fish').

### Thiere

Thiere or thiéré is a millet-based couscous and an important staple food in Senegal, The Gambia and Mauritania.

- From: Senegal
- Origin (Wikidata): Senegal
- Ingredients (Wikidata): millet, bean, meatball, potato, date pit

Fun facts:

- (history) Thiere was one of the main staple foods of the Senegambian region by at least the 15th century, when Europeans first arrived and left written records.
- (culture) Only in the 19th century did thiere begin to lose ground to thieboudienne, now Senegal's national dish.
- (origin) Thiere comes from Senegal.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Historically, saadj/thiere was produced by the Serer in the pre-colonial Serer Kingdoms of Sine, Baol, Saloum, and Tekrur as part of their normal dishes.
- (culture) Thiere was also prepared on Serer religious ceremonial occasions such as the Gamo, Raan, and Xooy festivals, as well as during weddings, naming ceremonies, etc.
- (culture) Historically the dish was prepared in a very labor-intensive way, with women pounding the millet by hand using a large traditional pestle and mortar and adding baobab leaf (laalo) to it to give it a smooth texture and enhance the taste.

### Café Touba

Café Touba is a coffee beverage that is a popular traditional drink from Senegal that is (more recently) also consumed in Guinea-Bissau, and is named for the city of Touba, Senegal.

- From: Senegal

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) Café Touba (French for 'Touba coffee') is named for the city of Touba, Senegal.
- (culture) The drink is traditionally consumed by the Islamic Mouride brotherhood as it came to Senegal when the brotherhood's founder, Sheikh Amadou Bamba Mbacké, returned from exile in Gabon in 1902.
- (about) Café Touba is a coffee drink that is flavored with grains of Selim or Guinea pepper (the dried fruit of the shrub Xylopia aethiopica) (locally known as djar, in the Wolof language) and sometimes cloves.

### Peanut stew

Peanut stew or groundnut stew, also known as maafe (Wolof mafé, maffé, maffe), and pate d'arachide (French), is a staple food stew in Western Africa.

- From: Senegal
- Origin (Wikidata): Senegal
- Ingredients (Wikidata): carrot, chicken, paper, maize, Allium sativum, leaf, Curcuma longa, Allium, Cuminum cyminum, okra

Fun facts:

- (origin) While maafe is a dish originating from Senegal, in Mali tigadéguéna is also a stew that originated from the Mandinka and Bambara people of Mali and served with Malian fufu (tuwo).
- (culture) Senegalese maafe is a favorite dish among several Senegalese Mauritanian and Gambian ethnic groups; it has become the national dish in Mali as well as a popular dish across West Africa, even outside West Africa such as in Cameroon and France.
- (about) Not to be confused with Indonesian peanut sauces.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Peanut stew comes from Senegal.
