### Bizcocho

Bizcocho is the name given in Uruguay and as factura in Argentina to a wide range of pastries, cakes or cookies.

- From: Uruguay

Fun facts:

- (about) From these studies and publications, it appears that the first bakers in Montevideo came from France and Spain, as documented in the immigration records of the first settlers in which their profession is recorded.
- (history) In fact, the puff pastry croissant, one of the varieties of bizcocho, was invented in France and its first recipe dates back to 1905.
- (about) Not to be confused with biskotso.

### Chajá

The chajá cake, or postre chajá, is a typical dessert in Uruguayan cuisine.

- From: Uruguay
- Origin (Wikidata): Uruguay
- Since: 1927-
- Named after: Southern Screamer
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cream, meringue, sponge cake, peach, Uruguayan cuisine

Fun facts:

- (history) Chajá was created on April 27, 1927 by Orlando Castellano, the owner of the Confitería Las Familias in the city of Paysandú.
- (origin) Chajá originated as a semi-industrialized confectionery, and is exported to Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and United States This dessert owes its name to the Southern screamer, a bird (locally known as the chajá) native to the central and southern parts of South America.
- (inspiration) Chajá is named after Southern Screamer.

### Chivito (sandwich)

Chivito is the national dish of Uruguay.

- From: Uruguay
- Origin (Wikidata): Uruguay

Fun facts:

- (history) In neighboring Argentina, chivito, barbecued kid, is a popular asado dish; it is reported that the Uruguayan chivito arose in Punta del Este, Uruguay, at a restaurant called "El Mejillón Bar" in 1946, when a woman from northern Argentina ordered a sandwich of chivito for a hurried, meal-expecting kid.

### Caruso sauce

Caruso sauce or salsa Caruso is a warm sauce in Uruguayan cuisine made of cream, ham, cheese, beef extract, and mushrooms, and sometimes nuts or onions.

- From: Uruguay
- Origin (Wikidata): Uruguay
- Since: 1954-
- Named after: Enrico Caruso
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cheese, cream, butter, ham, broth, edible mushroom

Fun facts:

- (history) Caruso sauce was created in the 1950s in Uruguay by Raymundo Monti of the restaurant Mario y Alberto in Montevideo.
- (inspiration) Caruso sauce was named in honor of the Neapolitan tenor Enrico Caruso who was popular in South America during his tours of the 1910s.
- (about) Several culinary seminars referred to Caruso sauce as "the new invention", and it gained international culinary recognition.

### Colonia cheese

Colonia cheese is a semi-hard cheese produced in Uruguay.

- From: Uruguay
- Origin (Wikidata): Uruguay
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cow's milk

Fun facts:

- (origin) Colonia cheese originated in 1868 in the city of Nueva Helvecia, founded in the early 1860s in the Colonia Department by Swiss immigrants.
- (about) Its name derives from its origins in the city of Nueva Helvecia, also known as Colonia Suiza (Swiss Colony), located within the country's main dairy-producing region in the southwest.
- (origin) Although it originated from Emmental cheese, it has a milder flavor and a softer consistency.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Colonia cheese originated as an attempt to reproduce Emmental cheese.
- (origin) Colonia cheese was first made in Municipality of Nueva Helvecia.
- (origin) Colonia cheese comes from Uruguay.
- (origin) A Swiss settler from the canton of Bern, Juan Teófilo Karlen initiated its production as a family enterprise in a region that subsequently developed into the country's main dairy-producing area, largely due to the significant settlement of Central European immigrants in the second half of the 19th century.

### Jesuita (sandwich)

A jesuita (as known in Uruguay) or fosforito (Argentina, lit. "matchstick") is a baked sandwich commonly eaten in parts of South America and considered a classic of Argentinian cuisine.

- From: Uruguay

Fun facts:

- (about) A similarly named sweet pastry known in Portugal, Spain and parts of South America, the jesuit, consists of puff pastry filled with custard.
- (culture) In Germany, Jesuitermützen are a custard-filled pastry traditionally cut into triangles.
- (culture) Although long commonly available in Argentinian bakeries, their popularity had waned until an interest in home baking and professional interest among pastry chefs in traditional dishes resurged in 2020.

### Uruguayan wine

Uruguayan wine was produced in the quantity of 102,964 tonnes from 9,023 hectares (22,300 acres) of vineyards in 2023, the fourth-greatest quantity in South America.

- From: Uruguay

Fun facts:

- (origin) In 1870, Albariño was introduced to Uruguay in 1954 by immigrants from A Coruña, in the Galician region of Spain.
- (history) When the Mercosur free trade association started to take shape in the late 1980s, Uruguay took steps to increase the quality of its wines and stepped up its marketing efforts, due to fear of being out-competed by Brazilian wines and Argentine wines, which had lower production costs.
- (origin) The modern wine industry in Uruguay dates back to 1870, when Tannat was introduced to the country by Don Pascual Harriague, a Basque.
