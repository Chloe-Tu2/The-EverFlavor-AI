### Kleicha

Kleicha (Persian: کلوچه, romanized: koluche; North Mesopotamian Arabic: كليچة, romanized: klēči, Mesopotamian Arabic: كليچة, romanized: kilēča; Najdi Arabic: كليجة, romanized: k(i)lēja; Syriac: ܟܠܫ̮ܵܐ, romanized: kleča; Kurmanji Kurdish: kade; Sorani Kurdish: کادە, romanized: kāda; Turkish: kiliçe) is a type of Middle Eastern cookie.

- From: Saudi Arabia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cardamom

Fun facts:

- (culture) Kleicha comes in several traditional shapes and fillings.
- (about) The most popular are the ones filled with dates (kleichat tamur).
- (culture) Muslims make kleicha for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha and also for their celebrations, weddings or special ceremonies and they are stuffed with many different fillings like dates, pistachios, walnuts, coconuts, dried figs, sesame seeds or Turkish delight.

### Tharid

Tharid also known as thareed, trid, fareed, tashrib, tashreeb or taghrib is a bread soup that originates from Mecca, Saudi Arabia, an Arab cuisine also found in many other Arab countries.

- From: Saudi Arabia
- Origin (Wikidata): Arabian Peninsula
- Ingredients (Wikidata): crumb

Fun facts:

- (culture) As an Arab national dish it is considered strongly evocative of Arab identity during the lifetime of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.
- (origin) Tharid comes from Arabian Peninsula.
- (culture) According to a widespread cultural tradition, this unremarkable and humble dish was the prophet's favorite food.

### Khaliat nahl

Khaliat al Nahl, also called Khaliat Nahl, or Honeycomb Bread in English, is an Arab dessert made of small, soft, cheese-filled bread rolls arranged in a pattern similar to hexagonal packing arrangement, resembling a honeycomb or beehive, and topped with nigella seeds and honey or syrup.

- From: Saudi Arabia
- Origin (Wikidata): Yemen, Oman, Arabian Peninsula

Fun facts:

- (origin) Khaliat nahl comes from Yemen, Oman, Arabian Peninsula.
