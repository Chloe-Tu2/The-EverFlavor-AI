### Bozbash

Bozbash is a traditional meat stew (also described as a soup) that is popular in Armenia, Azerbaijan and Iran.

- From: Azerbaijan
- Origin (Wikidata): Azerbaijan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): lamb meat

Fun facts:

- (origin) Ghanoonparvar notes that bozbash was introduced "relatively late" into Iranian cuisine; Mirza Ali-Akbar Khan, the chef of Naser al-Din Shah Qajar (r. 1848–1896), was reportedly the first to mention it.
- (about) According to William Pokhlyobkin, bozbash is the most popular Armenian soup.
- (origin) Bozbash comes from Azerbaijan.

### Piti (food)

Piti is a soup in the cuisines of the South Caucasus and its bordering nations, and is prepared in the oven in individual crocks with a glazed interior (called piti in Turkic languages).

- From: Azerbaijan
- Origin (Wikidata): Azerbaijan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): lamb meat, chickpea

Fun facts:

- (about) Piti is served in the crock, usually accompanied by an additional plate for "disassembling" the meat and the liquid part with vegetables, which may be eaten separately as the first (soup with veg.) and second (meat) course meal.
- (origin) Piti was first made in Caucasus.
- (about) Piti is a variety of abgoosht, particularly popular in Iran.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Piti comes from Azerbaijan.
- (about) Not to be confused with Francisco Medina Luna.

### Qutab

Qutab, or Gutab is a traditional dish in Azerbaijani, Turkmen and Turkish cuisine, made from thinly rolled dough that is filled with a variety of ingredients and cooked briefly on a convex griddle called a saj.

- From: Azerbaijan
- Origin (Wikidata): Azerbaijan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): beef

Fun facts:

- (about) This versatile and popular dish comes in many variations, with fillings ranging from savory meats, herbs, and pumpkin to more unconventional ingredients like minced sheep intestines.
- (culture) It holds cultural significance in Turkmenistan, Turkey and Azerbaijan, often enjoyed during family gatherings or celebrations.
- (origin) Qutab comes from Azerbaijan.

### Shekerbura

Shekerbura or shekarbura is an Azerbaijani sweet pastry, usually eaten as a dessert.

- From: Azerbaijan
- Origin (Wikidata): Azerbaijan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): hazelnut

Fun facts:

- (origin) Shekerbura was first made in Caucasus.
- (culture) Shekerbura, shorgoghal, and pakhlava are the traditional foods of Novruz in Azerbaijan.
- (culture) Like other pastries prepared for Novruz, shekerbura has a symbolic meaning which denotes the half-moon or flame of fire.
