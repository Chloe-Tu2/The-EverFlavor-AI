### Arany galuska

Arany galushka is a traditional Hungarian dessert consisting of balls of yeast dough (galuska).

- From: Hungary
- Origin (Wikidata): Hungary

Fun facts:

- (culture) The balls are rolled in melted butter, and then rolled in a mixture of sugar and crushed nuts (traditionally walnuts), assembled into layers, before being baked till golden.
- (about) Nancy Reagan popularized this dish in the United States when she served it at the White House for Christmas.
- (origin) Arany galuska comes from Hungary.

### Gulyásleves

Gulyásleves (gulyás 'herdsman' and leves 'soup' in Hungarian), is a Hungarian soup, made of beef, vegetables, ground paprika and other spices.

- From: Hungary
- Origin (Wikidata): Hungary
- Ingredients (Wikidata): beef, paprika, onion

Fun facts:

- (origin) It originates from a dish cooked by the cattlemen, who tended their herds in the Great Hungarian Plain (known as the Alföld or puszta in Hungarian).
- (culture) Traditional Hungarian bográcsgulyás 'cauldron goulash' is often still cooked outdoors over an open fire in a cauldron, giving the appearance of a barbecue.
- (about) Later on when the dish left the peasant cuisines and became popular even in the town, it started to be cooked more like a soup.

- Origin (Wikidata): Hungary
- Ingredients (Wikidata): pork

Fun facts:

- (about) Winter salami was first pioneered and popularized in Hungary by a wave of Friulian seasonal entrepreneurs and butchers in the second half of the nineteenth century, including József Meduna di Montecucco's "first Hungarian steamed salami and fat products" factory.
