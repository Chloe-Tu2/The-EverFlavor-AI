### Khachapuri

Khachapuri is a Georgian dish of cheese-filled bread.

- From: Georgia
- Origin (Wikidata): Georgia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): bread, cheese, flour, chicken egg

Fun facts:

- (culture) Khachapuri is Georgia's national dish, and the item "The Khachapuri Tradition in Georgia" was included in the list of Intangible Cultural Heritage elements in Georgia on January 22, 2019.
- (history) According to Darra Goldstein, who wrote the book The Georgian Feast about the dish, khachapuri probably dates back to the 12th century when Georgia experienced a period of renaissance, but its specific root remains unclear.
- (origin) Dali Tsatava, former professor at the Georgian Culinary Academy, suggested that khachapuri could be a "cousin of the pizza" as the concept of the dish might have been brought by Roman soldiers who crossed Europe, well before the addition of tomatoes in the 16th century.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Khachapuri is popular in Georgia, both in restaurants and as street food.
- (culture) Georgia celebrates "National Khachapuri Day" yearly on February 27.
- (origin) Khachapuri comes from Georgia.

### Lobio

Lobio is a traditional Georgian dish of various kinds of prepared beans (cooked or stewed), also containing coriander, walnuts, garlic and onion.

- From: Georgia
- Origin (Wikidata): Georgia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Phaseolus, bean, pomegranate

Fun facts:

- (origin) It is said to have originated in Georgia.
- (culture) One of the traditional recipes for lobio does not call for hot peppers (as other recipes do) but relies solely on ground black pepper for its spice.
- (origin) While the dish may predate any division of the lands into countries, the most popular variant today uses red kidney beans, which are native to the Americas and introduced into Georgia after 1500.

### Ajika

Ajika is a flavored sauce or dip made mainly in Abkhazia and Mingrelia regions (Georgia).

- From: Georgia
- Origin (Wikidata): Georgia, Abkhazia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): table salt, Allium sativum, tomato, vinegar, Capsicum, Khmeli suneli, paprika, Coriandrum sativum, chili pepper, onion

Fun facts:

- (about) In 2018, ajika was included on the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Georgia list.
- (culture) This distinction confirms that in the Mingrelian tradition, Ajika is not a synonym for salt, but a unique, processed condiment.
- (origin) Ajika comes from Georgia, Abkhazia.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) In the Abkhaz language, the condiment is formally known as apyrpyl-jika (pepper-salt), whereas the standalone term ajika became the standardized name for the specific Mingrelian preparation.
