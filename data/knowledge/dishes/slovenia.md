### Prekmurska gibanica

Prekmurska gibanica (Prekmurje layer pastry) is a type of Slovenian gibanica or layered pastry.

- From: Slovenia
- Origin (Wikidata): Slovenia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): flour

Fun facts:

- (culture) The oldest extant document (1828) by József Kossics described a wedding as never being held without gibanica, which is done as follows: "The dough is rolled until thin, sprinkled with grated cabbage, turnips, or quark.
- (about) Prekmurska gibanica is sprinkled as previously. 10 or 11 such layers are thus composed and form a conspicuous cake [sic]." Prekmurska gibanica was chosen to represent Slovenia in the Café Europe initiative of the Austrian presidency of the European Union, on Europe Day 2006.
- (origin) Prekmurska gibanica comes from Slovenia.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) For centuries, prekmurska gibanica was served as a festive and ritual dish in Prekmurje.
- (culture) Since March 2010, prekmurska gibanica is protected in the EU as a Traditional Speciality Guaranteed.

### Cviček

Cviček is a Slovenian wine from the Lower Carniola region of Slovenia.

- From: Slovenia

Fun facts:

- (about) Despite its long history of being known as a sour and poor wine, it has recently become a very popular drink with both local people and visitors to the region.
- (about) After gaining independence the people living in Lower Carniola began to defend the quality of the wine, and today it has become a popular casual drink for locals and visitors alike.
- (about) In the Middle Ages, the region which later became known as Lower Carniola was part of the Windic March (also Slovene or Slavonic March), often called simply "March".

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Its wine was thus named Marvin (from German Marwein from Markwein), which was also mentioned by Valvasor, a seventeenth-century Carniolan historian.

### Štruklji

Štruklji is a traditional Slovene dish, composed of dough and various types of filling.

- From: Slovenia

Fun facts:

- (history) The first recorded preparation of štruklji is said to be in 1589, when a chef at a manor in Graz wrote down the recipe for cooked štruklji with tarragon filling.
- (culture) Štruklji comes in the form of rolled dumplings, which can be steamed, boiled, or baked, and can have a wide range of fillings. Štruklji has been traditionally reserved for special occasions, but is now one of the most characteristic everyday dishes in households all across Slovenia.
- (history) Štruklji became a festive dish for the urban middle class in the 17th century, and spread to rural households two centuries later.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Štruklji was incorporated into everyday cuisine at the beginning of the 20th century.
- (culture) Štruklji is closely related to Zagorski štrukli, a traditional Croatian dish.
