### Bibikkan

Bibikkan is a traditional Sri Lankan coconut cake.

- From: Sri Lanka
- Origin (Wikidata): Sri Lanka

Fun facts:

- (origin) Bibikkan is believed to have been introduced to Sri Lanka by the Portuguese, who colonised the coastal areas of the island in 1505 and remained until 1658.
- (culture) Bibikkan is commonly prepared and consumed in celebration of festive and religious occasions, including Christmas, New Year's Eve, Sinhala and Tamil New Year.
- (culture) Goan Catholics serve a similar dish called Bebinca, which is almost identical to Bibingka, a traditional Filipino rice flour cake made with coconut milk, which is served on Christmas Day.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Bibikkan comes from Sri Lanka.

### Kokis

Kokis is a deep-fried, crispy Sri Lankan food made from rice flour and coconut milk.

- From: Sri Lanka

Fun facts:

- (culture) Although a traditional Sri Lankan dish, kokis is believed to be of Dutch origin from the time when parts of the country were under Dutch rule during the Kandyan period.
- (culture) Considered as a festive traditional Sri Lankan dish.
- (culture) Sinhalese people prepare and consume a number of traditional dishes, including kokis, to celebrate their new year in mid-April.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) This is an important snack when celebrating Sinhala New Year and plays a major role in the festivities.
- (culture) These are traditionally prepared by the women of the household, usually a few days before the new year.

### Bolo fiado

- From: Sri Lanka

### King coconut

King coconut is a dwarf coconut cultivar native to Sri Lanka, where it is known as Thæmbili also found in India and Indonesia.

- From: Sri Lanka

Fun facts:

- (about) There are several sub-varieties of the king coconut, the most common being the "red dwarf" (kaha thæmbili, commonly referred to as gon thæmbili).
- (about) The king coconut tree is a shorter variety, and is commonly found growing wild in many areas of the country.
- (about) The king coconut water, the liquid endosperm of young coconuts, is a nutritious beverage rich in sugars (mainly reducing sugars), minerals (mainly K+), vitamins (mainly B & C) and amino acids.

### Watalappam

Watalappam (also called watalappan or vattilappam) is a Sri Lankan coconut custard pudding made of coconut milk or condensed milk, jaggery, cashew nuts, eggs, various spices, including cardamom, cloves, and nutmeg, and sometimes thick pandan juice or grated vanilla pods.

- From: Sri Lanka
- Origin (Wikidata): Sri Lanka
- Ingredients (Wikidata): nutmeg, coconut milk, Pumpkin-coconut custard, cashew nut, cardamom

Fun facts:

- (origin) Watalappam is believed to have been brought to the country by Sri Lankan Malays in the 18th century, who moved from Indonesia to the country during Dutch rule.
- (culture) Watalappam is also popular during weddings, religious festivals and other social functions and celebrations.
- (about) Watalappam is more likely that the dessert is derived from a Malay dish known as serikaya, which is a steamed custard made from eggs, coconut milk, palm sugar and pandan or screwpine leaves.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Watalappam comes from Sri Lanka.
- (culture) The dessert has come to be strongly identified with Sri Lanka's Muslim community and is a part of a traditional Eid al-Fitr celebrations, marking the end of Ramadan.
