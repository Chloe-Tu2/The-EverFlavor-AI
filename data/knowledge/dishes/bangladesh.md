- (history) In some versions of the tale, the sweetmeat was prepared to commemorate her visit to India in 1856, while in other versions, it was prepared on the occasion of her birthday.
- (origin) The sweet originated in Kolkata in the middle of the 19th century.

- (inspiration) Ledikeni is named after Charlotte Canning, Countess Canning.

- (about) Ledikeni is very similar to pantua, another popular Bengali sweet.
- (about) As it gained popularity, it came to be known as "Lady Canning" which over time vernacularized into "ledikeni".
- (culture) Ledikeni remains a popular sweet in West Bengal and is still sold in the traditional sweet shops of Kolkata.

### Panta bhat

Panta bhat or poita bhat consists of cooked rice soaked and fermented in water.

- From: Bangladesh
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice

Fun facts:

- (history) Panta bhat has been described in documents from 17th century, while the dish Pakhala from Odisha documents back to 10th century CE, and is known as the origin of this dish.
- (culture) Panta bhat with Ilish (Hilsha) is the national dish of Bangladesh.
- (about) Panta bhat is a popular dish on the day of Pahela Baishakh or Bengali new year.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) It is unknown when Pakhala was first included in the daily diet of Eastern India, but it was included in the recipe of Lord Jagannath Temple of Puri circa 10th century.
- (history) Fray Sebastien Manrique reported from his visit of Bengal in 17th century that the people of all communities, according to Manrique, were contented then with the daily meal of rice, often panta bhat, salt and green vegetable (shak).
- (history) The word Pakhaḷa was used in the Odia poems of Arjuna Das in his literary work Kåḷpålåtā during 1520-1530 CE.
- (culture) Panta bhat is traditionally considered as beneficial in conditions.
- (about) In these times of polished rice, the popularity of rice varieties like Lal Swarna and White Swarna is often driven by their suitability for panta bhat.
- (about) The liquid part is known as Toraṇi in Odia.
- (culture) Traditionally served in the morning with salt, onion, chili and Aloo Makha/Alu Pitika (mashed potato).
