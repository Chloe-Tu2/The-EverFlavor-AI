### Gallo pinto

Gallo pinto or gallopinto is a traditional rice and bean dish from Central America.

- From: Nicaragua

Fun facts:

- (culture) Consisting of rice and beans as a base, gallo pinto is important to both Nicaragua and Costa Rica, both of which consider it a national dish.
- (about) According to anthropologist Teresa Preston-Werner, Fallas' "inclusion of pinto in the cultural landscape of the Caribbean coast demonstrates the meal’s ubiquity in daily life as early as the first part of the twentieth century".
- (origin) The name is said to originate in the multi-colored or speckled appearance that results from cooking the rice with black or red beans.

### Nacatamal

A nacatamal is a traditional Nicaraguan dish similar to the tamal and to the hallaca.

- From: Nicaragua
- Origin (Wikidata): Nicaragua

Fun facts:

- (origin) Nacatamales have Mesoamerican roots, and originated from the Nicarao tribes who inhabited western Nicaragua, its name originates from the Nawat language and translates to 'meat tamale'.
- (culture) The nacatamal is perhaps the most produced within traditional Nicaraguan cuisine and is typically eaten on the weekend, for dinner, or for breakfast; it is usually eaten together with white bread and a caffeinated drink like black coffee, Coca-Cola or Pepsi.
- (origin) After Spanish conquest, nacatamales were made with introduced meats like pork and chicken.

### Vigorón

Vigorón is a traditional Nicaraguan dish.

- From: Nicaragua
- Ingredients (Wikidata): chicharrón

Fun facts:

- (history) Dr. Alejandro Barberena Pérez, in his 1971 book Granada, stated that María Luisa Cisneros Lacayo, "La Loca", developed the recipe in 1914 in Granada, Nicaragua, and she named the dish "Vigoron" after seeing a poster advertising a then-contemporary medicinal tonic by that name (USPTO Serial 71068023).
- (culture) Vaho, a dish very similar to vigorón, is also a traditional Nicaraguan dish.

### Kola Shaler

Kola Shaler is a cola soft drink manufactured in Nicaragua, invented by David Robleto Alemán in 1904.

- From: Nicaragua
- Origin (Wikidata): Nicaragua
- Since: 1904-

Fun facts:

- (history) Kola Shaler was launched in 1904 by David Robleto Alemán, in his native city of León, Nicaragua.
- (about) Kola Shaler has been described as one of the "pioneers" in the soft drink industry in Nicaragua due to its longevity and continued popularity.
- (origin) Kola Shaler comes from Nicaragua.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) In 1925, Kola Shaler moved production to Managua, the country's capital.
- (history) Kola Shaler dates from 1904-.
- (history) The original Managua factory withstood one earthquake, in 1931, before being ultimately destroyed in the 1972 Nicaraguan earthquake, which leveled an estimated 70% of the city.
- (about) Robleto Alemán devised the name "Shaler", intending it to resonate with the popular soft drinks of the era, most of which bore English or German monikers.
- (about) In July 2022, the Kola Shaler factory was recognized as a historic business by Reyna Rueda, the Mayor of Managua.
- (about) As of 2022, the factory continues to be operational.
- (about) Since 2004, the drink's recipe remains the same till date.

### Pinolillo

Pinolillo is a traditional, nutrient-dense cornmeal and cacao-based drink that has been consumed in Nicaragua and Costa Rica for centuries, including by indigenous people before European contact.

- From: Nicaragua
- Origin (Wikidata): Nicaragua
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cornmeal

Fun facts:

- (culture) Pinolillo is traditionally served out of an artisanal gourd called a jícara or cumbo, which is made from the hard shell of the jicaro fruit.
- (origin) Pinolillo comes from Nicaragua.
- (about) The popular saying is frequently used by the Nicaraguan co-president, Daniel Ortega, in his speeches.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Consumption of pinolillo is so widespread and traditional that the substance has become a symbol of Nicaraguan culture.
- (culture) The sweet drink is made of toasted corn, a small amount of roasted cacao, and spices such as cinnamon, allspice and cloves, traditionally ground with a mortar and pestle.
