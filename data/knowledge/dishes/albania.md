### Ballokume

Ballokume is an Albanian cookie originating in the city of Elbasan, which is popular throughout Albania and Albanian communities.

- From: Albania
- Origin (Wikidata): Albania

Fun facts:

- (culture) Ballokume is traditionally eaten on Dita e Verës, an Albanian pagan holiday celebrated on 14 March.
- (origin) Tradition holds that the name comes from a 16-century Ottoman ruler of Elbasan who, after having tasted a small cake made from corn flour cooked in a wood fire, exclaimed: "Është ba si llokume!" ("It's as good as a lokum!"), the word ballokume resulting by contracting this sentence.
- (culture) Ballokume is traditionally kneaded in a copper bowl, which is supposed to improve the texture of the dough.

### Kabuni

Kabuni is a traditional Albanian dessert.

- From: Albania
- Origin (Wikidata): Albania
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice

Fun facts:

- (history) Kabuni is made of rice fried in butter, mutton broth (ram's neck only), raisins (rinsed first in warm water), and some salt.

### Tavë kosi

Tavë kosi ("yogurt casserole"), also known as Tavë Elbasani, is a traditional Albanian baked dish consisting of mutton and rice covered with a mixture of yogurt, eggs, butter and flour before being baked in the oven.

- From: Albania
- Origin (Wikidata): Albania
- Ingredients (Wikidata): soured milk, lamb meat

Fun facts:

- (origin) Thought to originate from the city of Elbasan, tavë kosi is regarded as one of the national dishes of Albania.
- (about) Chicken may be used as an alternative to mutton, in which case the dish is known as "tavë kosi me mish pule".
- (about) Tavë kosi is also popular in neighboring countries, including Greece, Kosovo, North Macedonia and Turkey, where it is commonly known as "Elbasan tava", a name derived from its place of origin.

### Trileçe

Trileçe is a popular dessert in Southeastern Europe, particularly in Albania, Turkey, Bosnia & Herzegovina, Serbia and Kosovo and other Balkan countries.

- From: Albania

Fun facts:

- (about) It consists of a light sponge cake soaked in a mixture of three types of milk (evaporated milk, condensed milk and whole milk), which gives it its name—derived from the Spanish tres leches (three milks), a dessert popular in Latin America.
- (origin) There is also the theory that Renata Casadei, an Italian chef at Piazza in Tirana, introduced it to Albania in the early 2000s after visiting a friend in the Dominican Republic.
- (history) Another theory was the packaging of condensed milk in the 1940s showcasing recipes for tres leches.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) One theory posits that the popularity of Latin American soap operas in Albania led local chefs to reverse-engineer the tres leches cake, which then spread to Turkey, Bosnia and Herzegovina and throughout the Balkans.

### Lakror

Lakror is a traditional and common regional Albanian pie dish of Albania made with different fillings consisting of various vegetables or meat.

- From: Albania

Fun facts:

- (culture) This traditional pie dish is also found among Albanian communities of southern Italy (Arbëreshë).
- (culture) Korçë and its surrounding areas have preserved a particular tradition of the lakror, considered a specialty by local Albanians.
