### Haminados

Haminados is a Sephardic Jewish dish of eggs that are cooked slowly until the whites turn red-brown and the yolks darken.

- From: Israel
- Named after: cholent
- Ingredients (Wikidata): egg as food

Fun facts:

- (history) Haminados have been a component of Jewish cuisine since at least the 15th century in Medieval Spain.
- (inspiration) Haminados is named after cholent.
- (about) Haminados is also known as huevos haminados, chaminados, or braised eggs.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Today, they are an integral part of Israeli cuisine, typically prepared either on their own or as part of the Sephardic and Mizrahi Shabbat stew known as chamin (a counterpart of Ashkenazi Cholent).
- (culture) The origins of haminados, or braised eggs, are rooted in the Jewish culinary traditions of Medieval Spain.
- (culture) Hélène Jawhara Piñer notes that during the Spanish Inquisition, traditional Jewish foods such as haminados were used as evidence against New Christians (converted Jews) for secretly adhering to Judaism.
- (culture) For instance, in 1490, Pedro Martínez from Soria was denounced for consuming red eggs during Lent, a period that coincided with the Jewish holiday of Passover.
- (history) Later, in 1505, Alvaro de Luna was tried in Almazán after witnesses reported that he ate red eggs on Fridays and during Lent.
- (culture) During this period, eggs were a staple in daily diets and held significant symbolic meaning within Jewish culture, representing various aspects of life such as creation, mourning, and celebration.

### Israeli salad

Israeli salad is a chopped salad of finely diced tomato, onion, cucumber, and bell or chili peppers.

- From: Israel
- Origin (Wikidata): Israel
- Ingredients (Wikidata): tomato, olive oil, lemon juice, cucumber

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) In the 1940, Kibbutz Beit Alfa pioneered a variety of cucumber named after the Kibbutz, which became the standard Israeli cucumber used in this salad.
- (origin) Food historian Gil Marks describes how Jewish immigrants in the late 19th century first encountered the cucumber and tomato salad in then-Ottoman Palestine, and traces its presence there back to the Turkish çoban salatası (shepherd's salad).
- (culture) Israeli salad has been described as the "most well-known national dish of Israel", and is a standard accompaniment to most Israeli meals.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Israeli salad was adopted by Jewish immigrants to the Levant in the late 19th century, who found the locally grown Kirby cucumbers and tomatoes in popular local salad.
- (about) Salads following similar recipes, with different names, are widespread and popular throughout the Eastern Mediterranean.
- (about) Israeli salad was popularized in the kibbutzim, where the Jewish farmers had local fresh produce at hand.
- (about) Adopted and popularized by the kibbutzim across the land, this salad latter migrated to all areas of the Israeli cuisine.
- (origin) Israeli salad comes from Israel.
- (origin) Although cucumbers have an ancient history in the Middle East, tomatoes, native to South America, were not cultivated in the Syria region until possibly as late as the 19th century, during the Ottoman Empire.

### Malawach

Malawach or melawwaḥ, is a Jewish Yemenite flatbread that is traditional in Yemeni cuisine and Israeli cuisine.

- From: Israel
- Origin (Wikidata): Israel, Yemen
- Ingredients (Wikidata): margarine, sugar, table salt, puff pastry, smen

Fun facts:

- (origin) According to Rabbi Gil Marks, a Jewish food historian, malawach is made from the same dough as jachnun, and both originated as a variation of puff pastry, brought to Yemen by Jews expelled from Spain.
- (culture) Malawach is traditionally served with hard-boiled eggs, zhug, and resek agvaniyot.
- (culture) The dough traditionally takes three days to make, since it is a multi-stage process, and the dough must rest in between each phase.

### Oroblanco

The oroblanco, oro blanco, or sweetie (Citrus grandis Osbeck × C. paradisi Macf.) is a citrus hybrid, resulting from a cross between an acidless pomelo and a Marsh grapefruit.

- From: Israel

Fun facts:

- (origin) The oroblanco was first created as the result of a cross between an acidless pomelo (C. grandis Osbeck) and the Marsh grapefruit (C. paradisi Macf.) carried out in April 1958 by Robert Soost and James W. Cameron, geneticists at the University of California, Riverside.
- (history) Oroblanco was later released to growers in 1980, who rapidly adopted it.
- (inspiration) The hybrid was first created by two geneticists at the University of California, Riverside, James W. Cameron and Robert Soost, in April 1958.

### Tzfatit

Tzfatit, or Tzfat cheese is a semi-hard salty cheese produced in Israel, originally from sheep's milk.

- From: Israel
- Origin (Wikidata): Israel
- Ingredients (Wikidata): sheep milk

Fun facts:

- (history) Tzfatit was first produced in the Jewish community of Safed (Tzfat in Hebrew) in 1840 and is still produced there by descendants of the original cheese makers.
- (origin) Tzfatit comes from Israel.
- (inspiration) Trimming is mainly based on the action of enzymes contained in a special ferment and on calcium chloride rather than on the action of lactic acid.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) The cheese was first produced around 1840 at HaMeiri Dairy, established in the home of Meir Arzoni (later HaMeiri), who immigrated to the Holy Land from Qajar, Iran.
