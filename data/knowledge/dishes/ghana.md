### Akpeteshie

Akpeteshie is a liquor produced by distilling palm wine or sugar cane, primarily in the region of Western Africa.

- From: Ghana
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana
- Ingredients (Wikidata): palm wine

Fun facts:

- (history) Despite being outlawed, illicit spirits remained commonplace, with reports that even schoolboys were able to easily obtain akpeteshie through the 1930s.
- (about) In Nigeria it is known as Ògógóró (Ogog'), an Urhobo word, usually distilled locally from fermented Raffia palm tree juice, where it is known as the country's homebrew.
- (culture) Today, there is a misconception that Ogogoro can be pure ethanol, but traditionally, it had to come from the palm tree and then be distilled from this source.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Akpeteshie is popular throughout West Africa, and goes by many names including apio, ogoglo, ogogoro (Ogog'), VC10, Kill Me Quick, Efie Nipa, Kele, Kumepreko, Anferewoase, Apiatiti, Home Boy, Nana Drobo, One Touch among others.
- (about) Akpeteshie is also known as sapele water, kparaga, kai-kai, Sun gbalaja, egun inu igo meaning The Masquerade in the Bottle, push-me-push-you, and/or crim-kena, sonsé ("do you do it?" in Yoruba language).
- (about) In the Igbo language it is known as Akpuru achia.
- (origin) Akpeteshie comes from Ghana.
- (history) Before the advent of European colonization of what is today Ghana, the Anlo brewed a local spirit also known as "kpótomenui," meaning "something hidden in a coconut mat fence." With British colonization of what became known as the Gold Coast, such local brewing was outlawed in the early 1930s.

- (about) The first factory was established in the Volta Region, taking advantage of the area's supply of sugar cane plantations.

### Aprapransa

Aprapransa, originally called 'akplijii' (or 'akplidzi') is a Ghanaian GaDangme(or Ga) dish prepared by heat mixing (or blending) roasted corn flour with palm nut soup.

- From: Ghana
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cornmeal

Fun facts:

- (culture) Aprapransa is also an ancient sacred soup of the GaDangme people and is always prepared as a complement to another sacred corn flour meal called Kpokpoi (or Kpekpele) during an Ancient Religious Hebraic Harvest Festival called Homowo.
- (origin) Aprapransa comes from Ghana.
- (culture) Aprapransa is usually served by the Akan tribe on special occasions such as marriage ceremonies, naming ceremonies, birthday celebrations, family cookouts, etc.

### Tubaani

Tubaani, also referred to as steamed black-eyed peas' pudding, is a popular Ghanaian dish that is commonly eaten in the northern regions and Zongo communities of Ghana.

- From: Ghana
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Bambara groundnut, water, black-eyed pea, cassava flour

Fun facts:

- (about) The dish consists of a paste made from the flour of black-eyed peas and water which is then cooked after being first wrapped in the sweet-tasting, aromatic leaves of the Marantaceous herb Thaumatococcus daniellii and served with gravy or pepper and sliced onions tossed in hot vegetable oil.
- (origin) Tubaani comes from Ghana.
