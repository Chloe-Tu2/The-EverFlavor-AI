### Omo tuo

### Kokonte

Kokonte, also known as abetiɛ, lapiiwa, lapelawa or “face the wall”, is a staple swallow food eaten in some parts of Africa including Togo, Ghana and others.

- From: Ghana
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana
- Ingredients (Wikidata): drinking water, table salt, cassava flour, water

Fun facts:

- (about) Kokonte is popular in West African countries such as Nigeria and is also eaten in the Caribbean.
- (origin) Kokonte comes from Ghana.
- (about) Kokonte is similar to tapioca, a popular Brazilian dish that is also popular in the Volta Region of Ghana.

### Tubaani

Tubaani, also referred to as steamed black-eyed peas' pudding, is a popular Ghanaian dish that is commonly eaten in the northern regions and Zongo communities of Ghana.

- From: Ghana
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Bambara groundnut, water, black-eyed pea, cassava flour

Fun facts:

- (about) The dish consists of a paste made from the flour of black-eyed peas and water which is then cooked after being first wrapped in the sweet-tasting, aromatic leaves of the Marantaceous herb Thaumatococcus daniellii and served with gravy or pepper and sliced onions tossed in hot vegetable oil.
- (origin) Tubaani comes from Ghana.
