### Kisra

Kisra, also spelled kissra, is a Sudanese popular thin fermented bread made in Sudan, Chad, South Sudan, Algeria and some parts of Uganda and Kenya.

- From: South Sudan
- Origin (Wikidata): Kenya, Algeria, Chad, South Sudan, Uganda, Sudan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Sorghum

Fun facts:

- (history) As of 1995, the then-undivided country of Sudan ate an estimated 20,000 to 30,000 short tons (18,000 to 27,000 t) of sorghum flour annually in kisra.
- (about) There are two different forms of kisra: thin baked sheets, known as kisra rhaheefa, which is similar to injera; and a porridge known as kisra aseeda or aceda.
- (origin) Kisra comes from Kenya, Algeria, Chad, South Sudan, Uganda, Sudan.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) In 2025, CNN listed kisra as one of the world's top 50 breads.
