### 'otai

ʻOtai is a refreshing Polynesian beverage, typically made with coconut meat, coconut milk, and coconut water.

- From: Tonga
- Origin (Wikidata): Tonga, Samoa, Wallis and Futuna
- Ingredients (Wikidata): drinking water, fruit, coconut

Fun facts:

- (history) European colonists documented the Samoan version of ʻOtai in the late 19th century.
- (about) The coconut water and natural enzymes from the fruits used in the drink are believed to aid digestion, making it not only a refreshing beverage but also a beneficial one for digestive health.
- (culture) History of ʻOtai The drink has its origins in Western Polynesia, where it was traditionally served as a refreshing accompaniment to large meals.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) In Samoa, ʻOtai refers specifically to the version made with vi fruit, while versions made with introduced fruits are referred to by their respective names: vai meleni (watermelon drink), vai mago (mango drink), and vai fala (pineapple drink).
- (origin) 'otai comes from Tonga, Samoa, Wallis and Futuna.
- (about) The original recipe involved mixing grated ambarella fruit (known as vi in Samoan and Tongan and wi in Hawaiian) with young coconut meat, coconut milk, and coconut water.
- (origin) Tongan historians note that this modern recipe is a departure from the traditional Polynesian version, as many ingredients—such as milk, refined sugar, and introduced fruits like watermelon, mango, and pineapple—were not native to Tonga.
- (culture) While Samoa traditionally used ambarella (vi), Tonga used the mountain apple (fekika).
- (origin) In Samoa, the distinction between native and introduced recipes remains important.
