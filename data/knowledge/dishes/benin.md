### Amiwo

Amiwo is a traditional dish in Benin, consisting of a savory pudding made from spices (onions, garlic, black pepper, salt), cornflour and tomato paste, cooking oil or more traditionally palm oil.

- From: Benin
- Origin (Wikidata): Benin
- Ingredients (Wikidata): palm oil, tomato paste, onion, garlic, maize flour

### Bomiwô

Bomiwô is a traditional dish from Benin, particularly associated with the Fon cultural areas in the southern part of the country.

- From: Benin

Fun facts:

- (culture) In 2021, the commune of Bohicon presented Bomiwô as a representative culinary specialty during its participation in the UNESCO Creative Cities Network initiative focused on gastronomy.
- (origin) The word Bomiwô comes from the Fon language (fongbe), where it is commonly interpreted as “pâte des gris-gris” or “fetish paste.” According to oral traditions, the dish was historically associated with Vodun religious practices and was once reserved for ancestors and sacred ceremonies in Vodun convents.
- (culture) Bomiwô is a maize-based paste prepared similarly to amiwô, another well-known Beninese dish, but differs in color, symbolism, and cultural significance.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Bomiwô has strong cultural and spiritual importance within Fon society and Vodun traditions.
- (culture) Contemporary chefs and cultural promoters have highlighted its symbolic value as part of Benin's culinary heritage.

### Sodabi

Sodabi is a homemade alcohol obtained by distilling palm wine.

- From: Benin

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) While this version of the story is likely false, it remains the most popular explanation for the origin of the name "Sodabi." The second theory attributes the name Sodabi to its inventors, the Sodabi brothers.
- (history) In 1931, the colonial administration decided to ban sodabi production, officially for health reasons.
- (culture) Sodabi is a traditional beverage in many tropical regions and is widely consumed in West Africa.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) The history of sodabi belongs to oral tradition, which is why there are multiple hypotheses regarding its origins.
- (culture) Sodabi is also common to offer a glass of sodabi, or sometimes even a whole bottle, as a token of thanks for a favor or to celebrate the signing of a contract.
- (culture) Sodabi is an integral part of the Vodun religion; indeed, it is used repeatedly in rituals.
- (about) Two main theories are frequently mentioned: According to the first theory, an Englishman doubted that Beninese people could produce an alcohol similar to gin.
- (history) This second attempt was a resounding success, and the technique of making alcohol from palm wine quickly spread throughout Benin and Togo in the 1930s.
- (about) The first glass is sometimes poured onto the ground, accompanied by a few words of prayer or a blessing.
- (history) These two Beninese men from Allada were enlisted in the French colonial regiments during World War I. During their time in Europe, they learned distillation techniques, which they brought back to Benin after the 1918 armistice.
- (about) They built a homemade still and attempted their first distillation using fermented bananas.

### Wagasi

Wagasi is a type of West African cheese made from cow's milk.

- From: Benin
- Origin (Wikidata): Benin
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cow's milk

Fun facts:

- (history) It is commonly known as wagasi in the Zarma-Songhai and Dendi languages, amo in the Fon language, wara and warakashi in Nagot and Yoruba language and gasaru in Bariba language.
- (about) In Ghana it is also widely known as wagashi.
- (origin) Wagasi comes from Benin.
