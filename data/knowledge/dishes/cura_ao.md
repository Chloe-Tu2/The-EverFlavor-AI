### Curaçao (liqueur)

Curaçao is a liqueur flavored with the dried peel of the bitter orange variety laraha, a citrus fruit grown on the Caribbean island of Curaçao.

- From: Curaçao
- Origin (Wikidata): Curaçao
- Named after: Curaçao
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Curaçao orange peel

Fun facts:

- (history) In 1912, Bols sold blue curaçao as Crème de Ciel ("cream of the sky"), most likely a reference to the 1907 musical Miss Hook of Holland.
- (about) Curaçao is not definitively known who developed the first curaçao liqueur, or when.
- (origin) According to the early nineteenth-century French culinary chronicler Alexandre Grimod de la Reynière, curaçao originated in Flanders, and proximity to the county Holland gave distillers easy access to the necessary peels (since Curaçao was a Dutch colony at the time).

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (inspiration) Curaçao is named after Curaçao.
- (culture) Curaçao liqueur is traditionally made with the dried peels of the laraha (Citrus × aurantium subsp. currassuviencis), a bitter orange that had selectively adapted to conditions on Curaçao island.
- (origin) Curaçao comes from Curaçao.
- (history) The Bols distillery, founded in 1575 in Amsterdam, held shares in both the West and East India Companies to ensure access to the spices required for its distilled drinks.
- (history) Spanish explorers had brought the progenitor of the laraha, the bitter Seville orange, to the island in 1527.
- (history) The family, Senior and Chumaceiro, started selling their liqueur in 1896 in their pharmacy in small quantities.
- (history) In 1947 they bought the landhuis ("country manor") Chobolobo in Willemstad, where the distillery has since been housed.
- (history) The Bols company says that Lucas Bols (1652–1719) developed a laraha-based liqueur after discovering that an aromatic oil could be extracted from the unripe peel of otherwise useless bitter oranges.

### Pastechi
