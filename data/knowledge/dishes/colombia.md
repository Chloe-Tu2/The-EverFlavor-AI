### Achira (biscuit)

Achiras or achira crackers are traditional snacks from Huila, and their consumption has diversified in the rest of Colombia.

- From: Colombia

Fun facts:

- (about) The rhizomes of the Canna indica plant were cooked or roasted, and consumed as food by the Chibchas in Colombia, but it is believed that their use spread throughout the Andes due to trade with other indigenous communities.
- (about) It melts slightly in your mouth with a crispy, soft and gritty texture, with a taste of milk.The achira is also known as saga or chisgua in Colombia.

### Bandeja paisa

Bandeja paisa, with variations known as bandeja de arriero, bandeja montañera, and bandeja antioqueña, is one of the most representative meals in Colombian cuisine, especially of the Antioquia department and the Paisa region, as well as with the Colombian Coffee-Growers Axis (the departments of Caldas, Quindío and Risaralda, Tolima and Valle del Cauca).

- From: Colombia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): kidney bean

Fun facts:

- (culture) The main characteristic of this dish is the generous amount and variety of food in a traditional bandeja paisa: red beans cooked with pork, white rice, carne molida (ground meat), chicharrón, fried egg, plantain (plátano maduro), chorizo, arepa, hogao sauce, black pudding (morcilla), avocado and lemon.

### Champús

Champús is a popular drink in South America, particularly Ecuador, Peru and southwest Colombia.

- From: Colombia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): maize

Fun facts:

- (origin) It is believed to have originated in southwestern Colombia.
- (about) Champús is made with corn, fruits (such as lulo, also known as naranjilla), pineapple, quince or guanábana, sweetened with panela and seasoned with cinnamon, cloves and orange tree leaves.
- (culture) It is common in funeral rites as many indigenous traditions consider the beverage a favorite of the dead.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) In the Department of Valle del Cauca it is served very cold, being popular at any time of the year.

### Hogao

Hogao is a variant of Spanish sofrito and is typically used in Colombian cuisine.

- From: Colombia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): onion, tomato, garlic, cumin seed, cilantro

Fun facts:

- (culture) Hogao is traditionally made with only long green onions and tomatoes, differing from guiso, which can also be made with round onions, garlic, cumin, salt, and pepper that are sauteed over low heat during the cooking process.

### Sago

Sago is a starch extracted from the soft, spongy core tissue of several tropical palm species, especially Metroxylon sagu.

- From: Colombia

Fun facts:

- (culture) For centuries, it has been an important traditional staple food among many indigenous communities across the islands of Southeast Asia and the Melanesian region, including Malaysia, Indonesia, and Papua New Guinea.
- (about) Among the Melanau people, sago flour is commonly known as "lemantak".
- (inspiration) A famous traditional food made from it is linut, a thick sticky paste created by pouring hot water into sago flour and stirring it until it becomes glue-like in texture.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Traditionally, the trunk of the sago palm is harvested before the tree fully matures because the starch reserves inside the trunk are richest at that stage.
- (culture) After settling and drying, the starch becomes sago flour, which can then be used in many traditional dishes.
