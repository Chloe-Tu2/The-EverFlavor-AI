### Aloo pie

An aloo pie is a Caribbean fast food dish common in Trinidad and Tobago and is of Indo-Trinidadian origin.

- From: Trinidad and Tobago
- Origin (Wikidata): Trinidad and Tobago
- Ingredients (Wikidata): pastry

Fun facts:

- (history) Trinidad was a British colony from 1797 to 1962, so English is spoken there.
- (inspiration) Aloo pie is seasoned with a chutney (usually based on tamarind or mango), chadon beni and a spicy pepper sauce.
- (origin) Aloo pie comes from Trinidad and Tobago.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) After the abolition of slavery in Trinidad, numerous Indians were recruited from 1845 as cheap labor for the plantations, who in turn introduced curries that were often vegetarian and sometimes contained potatoes.
- (inspiration) As a rule, the dumplings are cut open before serving and garnished with a curry based on chickpeas or green peas.

### Buljol

Buljol is a salad dish of the cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago.

- From: Trinidad and Tobago
- Origin (Wikidata): Trinidad and Tobago
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Gadus morhua

Fun facts:

- (history) The name is a combination of the French words brulé ('burnt') and gueule ('muzzle'), which was changed into bu'n jaw in Trinidad's 19th century patois and finally morphed into buljol.
- (origin) Buljol comes from Trinidad and Tobago.
- (history) The name is of French origin. 18th-century colonial power Spain launched the cédula de población in 1783, an edict that successfully promoted the settling of French (i.e. likewise Catholic) planters in Trinidad who quickly set the population majority.

### Doubles (food)

Doubles is a common street food originating in Trinidad and Tobago and is of Indo-Trinidadian origin.

- From: Trinidad and Tobago
- Origin (Wikidata): Trinidad and Tobago
- Ingredients (Wikidata): flour, Vada, chickpea

Fun facts:

- (history) Doubles as a dish was created in Princes Town by Emamool "Mamoodeen" Deen and his wife Raheeman Rasulan Deen in 1936.
- (about) Doubles is normally eaten during breakfast, but is also eaten occasionally during lunch or as a late-night snack and popular hangover food.
- (inspiration) Doubles has been speculated that it was inspired by a northern Indian dish called chole bhature (or sometimes channa bhatura).

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Given the diversity of Trinidad, doubles is credited with its ability to "define and maintain symbolic boundaries of identification", and is considered an authentic standard of Trinidadian cuisine.
- (about) Trinidadian-Canadian filmmaker Ian Harnarine has released two films, the 2011 short film Doubles with Slight Pepper and the 2023 feature expansion Doubles, which centre in part on the significance of doubles as a signifier of Trinidadian culture among Trinidadian expatriates.
- (origin) Doubles comes from Trinidad and Tobago.
- (about) Chole bhature is made by combining channa masala and bhature (poori), which is a fried bread made with all-purpose flour, also known as "maida" in Hindi.

### Roti (wrap)

Originating in the Caribbean with Indian roots, a roti is a wrap-style sandwich filled with either curried or sometimes stewed meats or vegetables wrapped inside a dhalpuri, paratha, or dosti roti.

- From: Trinidad and Tobago
- Origin (Wikidata): Trinidad and Tobago
- Ingredients (Wikidata): potato

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) The roti sandwich is named after the chapati bread (also known as roti) in which it is wrapped.
- (about) As Trinidadians immigrated to other countries, especially in North America and the United Kingdom, they brought with them the roti and opened roti shops to sell it.
- (origin) Roti comes from Trinidad and Tobago.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Indian indentured workers came to Trinidad in great numbers after the slavery system ended in 1838.
- (history) In 1937, a South Trinidadian businessman ran a snack bar in San Fernando.
- (about) The "invention" spread over Trinidad and subsequently over Guyana, Suriname, and Jamaica.

### Puncheon rum

- From: Trinidad and Tobago
- Origin (Wikidata): Trinidad and Tobago

- (about) The name 'Puncheon' is derived from the giant wooden casks, known as 'puncheons' in which the rum was stored.

### Saheena

Saheena (also spelled sahina or sahiena, ) is a street food and snack in the cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago.

- From: Trinidad and Tobago

Fun facts:

- (about) Mobile breakfast stalls that sell doubles, the most popular breakfast in Trinidad, often also sell saheena.
- (history) After the end of slavery in Trinidad in 1840, the plantations—which dominated the island's economy at the time—needed a cheap substitute for slave labour.
- (about) While many dishes from Indo-Trinidadian cuisine are popular on other Caribbean islands, saheena remains a dish prevalent in Trinidad only.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) From 1840 on, workers were recruited in large numbers who had to commit themselves for five years minimum and were promised five acres of land as a reward.
