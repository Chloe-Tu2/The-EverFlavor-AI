### Pupusa

A pupusa is a thick griddle cake or flatbread from El Salvador and Honduras made with cornmeal or rice flour stuffed with one or more ingredients including cheese, beans, chicharrón, or squash.

- From: El Salvador
- Origin (Wikidata): El Salvador
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice flour, cornmeal

Fun facts:

- (history) Pupusas have origins in Pre-Columbian Mesoamerica but were first mentioned in 1837 by Guatemalan poet José Batres Montúfar.
- (culture) The pupusa is the national dish of El Salvador.
- (about) In Lidia Pérez de Novoa's book Interlude and Other Verses, she believed that pupusa derives from the Nawat word pupusawa meaning "to puff up".

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Salvadoran anthropologist Ramón Rivas does not attribute the pupusa to any country in particular except to state that it originated in Mesoamerica in general.
- (origin) The earliest ancestral dish of the pupusa originated in the Pre-Columbian era in modern-day El Salvador, Honduras, and Guatemala as a thick corn tortilla.
- (history) Franciscan friar Bernardino de Sahagún wrote in 1570 that Indigenous Americans prepared a dish consisting of a mixture of dough, beans, and meat in Pre-Columbian Mesoamerica.
- (history) The earliest mention of modern pupusas was made by Guatemalan poet José Batres Montúfar in 1837.
- (history) When corn became scarce in the late 1930s, some Salvadorans replaced corn flour with rice flour to make pupusas.
- (culture) Pupusa can be served with curtido and tomato sauce and is traditionally eaten by hand.
- (inspiration) In El Salvador, the pupusa has a national holiday dedicated to it, which is celebrated on the second Sunday of November every year.
- (about) The pupusa's origin was discussed during negotiations for the Dominican Republic–Central America Free Trade Agreement (CAFTA–DR) in 2003.
- (about) In 2018, the World Trade Organization listed El Salvador as the pupusa's denominación de origen ("designation of origin").

- (about) This way of making pupusas, known as pupusas de arroz, began in Olocuilta.

- (about) Not to be confused with pu pu platter.

### Quesadilla Salvadoreña

Quesadilla salvadoreña, or Salvadoran quesadilla, is a pan dulce, similar to a pound cake, made with rice flour and queso duro blando and topped with sesame seeds, that is popular in El Salvador and eastern Guatemala.

- From: El Salvador

Fun facts:

- (history) By the 21st century the dish was increasingly found in US cities with large Salvadoran diaspora populations, such as San Antonio, New York, and Los Angeles.
- (culture) Quesadilla Salvadoreña is traditionally baked on Sunday mornings.
