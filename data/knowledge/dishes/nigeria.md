### Abacha (food)

Abacha () is a type of food originating with the Igbos of Nigeria.

- From: Nigeria
- Origin (Wikidata): Nigeria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): vegetable, table salt, palm oil, crayfish as food, peppercorn, onion, Ogiri, Oil Bean Soup, garden egg sauce

Fun facts:

- (culture) Palm oil, which may either be red or a combination of red and bleached, is curdled using either kaun (potash), ngu (a traditional version of potash) or baking soda, and the abacha slices are then mixed in the curdled oil.
- (origin) Abacha comes from Nigeria.
- (about) Shredded dried cassava first needs to be softened by soaking in boiled water and covered up for a few minutes.

### Akara

Akara is a type of fritter made from cowpeas or beans (black-eyed peas) that originated in Yorubaland, from the Yoruba ethnic group inhabiting parts of Nigeria, Benin and Togo.

- From: Nigeria
- Origin (Wikidata): Ghana, Nigeria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): table salt, Capsicum, palm oil, black-eyed pea, onion

Fun facts:

- (history) Akara is documented to also mean a variety of both bean/cowpea and non cowpea fritter in "A vocabulary of the Yoruba language" published 1852.
- (culture) Akara is the ancestor of Acarajé, traditionally found in Brazil's northeastern state of Bahia, especially in the city of Salvador.
- (origin) Akara comes from Ghana, Nigeria.
