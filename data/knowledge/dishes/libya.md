### Bazin (bread)

Bazin alternatively recognized as Bazeen, Bézine or Bazine is an unleavened bread in the cuisine of Libya prepared with barley, water and salt.

- From: Libya
- Origin (Wikidata): Libya
- Ingredients (Wikidata): water, table salt, barley

Fun facts:

- (culture) Bazin has been described as a traditional dish and as a national dish of Libya.
- (origin) Bazin comes from Libya.

### Mafrum

Mafrum, also spelled "mafroom", is a Libyan Jewish stuffed vegetable dish.

- From: Libya
- Origin (Wikidata): Tunisia, Libya
- Ingredients (Wikidata): potato, minced meat

Fun facts:

- (history) It spread beyond Libya due to the mass Jewish exodus from the country in the 1950s and 60s.
- (origin) In Italy, mafrum was introduced to the Jewish community of Rome after the evacuation of Libyan Jews following the Six-Day War in 1967.
- (culture) Mafrum is traditionally served with couscous and eaten during Shabbat and Jewish holidays.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) In Israel, it became a popular dish, with interpretations by Jews from Tunisia, Morocco, and Egypt.
- (origin) Mafrum comes from Tunisia, Libya.

### Libyan tea

Libyan tea is a strong beverage, prepared with traditional set of stainless steel utensils and served in a small glass cups.

- From: Libya
- Origin (Wikidata): Libya

Fun facts:

- (origin) According to one interpretation, the characteristic foam that forms on the surface of Libyan tea may have originated as a practical adaptation to desert conditions.
- (culture) The tea is traditionally served as part of a ceremonial presentation known as Shahi al-Alla.
- (culture) This ritual involves the use of a distinctive set of stainless steel utensils, including a teapot, a kettle, serving trays, glass cups, and containers for sugar.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Over time, this functional feature became an established and valued element of the tea's traditional preparation and presentation.
- (origin) Libyan tea comes from Libya.
