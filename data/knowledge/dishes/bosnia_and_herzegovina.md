### Bosnian pot

Bosnian pot (Bosanski lonac) is a Bosnian stew, a culinary speciality, appreciated for its rich taste and flexibility.

- From: Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Origin (Wikidata): Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Ingredients (Wikidata): carrot, meat, potato, Allium sativum, beef, mutton

Fun facts:

- (culture) Bosnian pot has been described as a national dish of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

### Livno cheese

The Livno cheese is a cheese first produced in the 19th century in the area of Livno, Bosnia and Herzegovina, where the cheese gets its name.

- From: Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Origin (Wikidata): Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cow's milk

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) Production started in 1886 when Austro-Hungarian administrators introduced method of the production based on French technology of making the Gruyère cheese, bringing Swiss cheese producers to train chosen local producers.
- (origin) Livno cheese comes from Bosnia and Herzegovina.

### Uštipci

Uštipci are doughnut-like fried dough balls popular in Southeast European countries, namely Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, and Slovenia.

- From: Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Origin (Wikidata): Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Ingredients (Wikidata): yeast

Fun facts:

- (about) In Bosnia and Herzegovina, there exists a non-yeast variant of uštipci known as peksimeti; and an unleavened variant known as lokumi (not to be confused with the Bosnian shortbread known by the same name).
- (origin) Uštipci comes from Bosnia and Herzegovina.
- (about) In Croatia, a variant of the dish known as fritule is also made; the two names are commonly used interchangeably for both dishes.

### Vlašić cheese

Vlašić cheese or Travnik cheese is a brined mostly low-fat white cheese made from sheep-milk.

- From: Bosnia and Herzegovina
- Origin (Wikidata): Bosnia and Herzegovina

Fun facts:

- (origin) In its 1953 handbook "Cheese Varieties", the United States Department of Agriculture described Travnik cheese as originating in Albania in the early 19th century, where it was known as Arnautski sir.
- (culture) Henceforth, by perfecting the recipe, the Vlachs passed on the tradition to other cattle breeders from the surrounding mountains.
- (about) The cheese later spread to Yugoslavia, where it became known as Travnik.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):
