### Pizza in North Korea

North Korea has several restaurants serving pizza.

- From: North Korea

Fun facts:

- (history) North Korean leader Kim Jong Il, who was noted for his lavish culinary habits, made efforts to introduce international cuisine in the 1990s.
- (origin) Kim Jong Il hired Italian chefs to train North Koreans in pizza making and introduced it to the country.
- (about) Furlanis described his experience in a three-part series for Asia Times Online in August 2001.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) In the spring of 1997, he had pizzaiolo Ermanno Furlanis flown into Pyongyang, along with another Italian chef and both of their wives, to train North Korean chefs in pizza-making for three weeks.

### Pyeonsu

Pyeonsu is a square-shaped mandu (dumpling) in Korean cuisine.

- From: North Korea
- Origin (Wikidata): North Korea

Fun facts:

- (origin) Although Korean mandu are believed to have first been brought to Korea by Yuan Mongolians in the 14th century during the Goryeo, the variety pyeonsu is thought to be derived from Ming Chinese dumplings brought into the Korean peninsula by the Joseon merchants based in Gaeseong.
- (history) The 1809 compendium Women's Encyclopedia records that pyeonsu of Songdo (name of Gaeseong at the time) is famous and is made during the festivities around Korean New Year.
- (about) The city of Gaeseong is famous for its pyeonsu.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Some claim that it was derived from byeonsi, which appears in the 1527 book Collection of Characters for Training the Unenlightened as a borrowing from the Old Mandarin Chinese biǎnshí that meant "dumplings" at the time.
- (origin) Another claim is that the word was derived from Byeon-ssi mandu, which means "Byeon's dumplings", because it was first made by a person whose family name was Byeon.
- (history) Unlike other dumpling varieties, pyeonsu was not popularized on a nationwide scale during the 20th century and remains a regional specialty usually served only in Gaeseong-style restaurants.
- (history) In Korean royal court cuisine during the Joseon era (1392–1897), wheat-skin dumplings boiled in water were called byeongsi, with the letters byeong and si which, combined, could refer to dumplings that are eaten with a spoon.
- (history) During the Joseon era (1392–1897), wheat flour was a rare and expensive ingredient in most regions, except in Hwanghae Province, where wheat was very common.
- (origin) Pyeonsu comes from North Korea.

### Taedonggang

Taedonggang is a brand of North Korean beer brewed by the state-owned Taedonggang Brewing Company based in Pyongyang.

- From: North Korea

Fun facts:

- (about) Not to be confused with Taedonggang.
- (about) In 2000, Kim Jong Il decided the country needed a showcase brewery.
- (about) Reinstalled and operational from 2002, the brewery uses German-made computerized brewing control technology.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) On 3 July 2009, a commercial for the product was broadcast on state-run Korean Central Television in a rare move, as there are very few advertisements on North Korean television.
- (about) Since 2016, the beer has been available in China in limited amounts.
