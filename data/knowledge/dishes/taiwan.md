### Ageh (food)

Ageh or Tamsui ageh is a speciality food originating from Tamsui District of New Taipei City and consists of a piece of fried tofu, stuffed with cooked cellophane noodles, and sealed with surimi, which is widely sold by vendors in the district.

- From: Taiwan
- Origin (Wikidata): Taiwan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cellophane noodles, fried tofu

Fun facts:

- (culture) The ageh was reportedly created in 1965 by Iông-tēⁿ Gím-bûn, who combined various food items sold at her Chin-lí street food stall to sell as a new food item.
- (origin) Ageh comes from Taiwan.

### Bah-uân

Bah-uân is a Taiwanese dumpling made of starch and rice flour, filled with meat and vegetables.

- From: Taiwan
- Origin (Wikidata): Taiwan

Fun facts:

- (history) It is believed that ba-wan were first prepared in the Beidou township of Changhua County by a scribe by the name of Fan Wan-chu as food for disaster relief, when the region was struck by heavy floods in 1898.
- (culture) Bah-uân is gelatinous and translucent, and often considered street food.
- (about) In the township of Lukang, Changhua County, ba-wan are known as bahhoe because they take on the block-like shape of the character 回.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Bah-uân comes from Taiwan.
- (culture) The traditional wrapper was made with sweet potato starch alone, sweet potatoes were the dominant food crop in pre-1950s Taiwan and were traditionally preserved by extracting their starch.

### Braised pork rice

Braised pork rice is a gaifan dish found in Fujianese cuisine and Taiwanese cuisine.

- From: Taiwan
- Origin (Wikidata): Taiwan

Fun facts:

- (origin) Braised pork belly likely originated from Quanzhou, China, and was brought to Taiwan by immigrants during the Qing Dynasty.
- (about) Although subject to regional variations, dishes are typically made of pork belly cooked in a process known as lǔ 滷 (boiled and marinated in soy sauce and sugar) and served on top of rice.
- (about) Braised pork rice is a popular comfort food in Taiwan, with many street vendors and night markets offering their own secret recipes and variations.

- (about) Minced pork rice is instead named bah-sò-pn̄g in the south.

### Cinavu

Cinavu is a Taiwanese traditional millet-based dumpling prepared by the indigenous Paiwan people of southern Taiwan.

- From: Taiwan
- Origin (Wikidata): Taiwan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Setaria italica, Trichodesma calycosum

Fun facts:

- (culture) Cinavu is traditionally offered in important social gatherings of the Paiwan people, such as weddings and festivals, as well as a packed lunch during hunting or agricultural work, like a sandwich.
- (origin) Cinavu comes from Taiwan.
- (about) Not to be confused with zongzi.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Cinavu was included in the 2024 Taiwan President Lai Ching-te's inaugural state banquet as one of Taiwan's significant national dishes.
- (culture) Unlike zongzi, which is only eaten during the Dragon Boat Festival, cinavu can be eaten any time during the year.

### Iron egg

Iron egg is a special version of soy egg, a snack from Taiwan.

- From: Taiwan

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) Although Yang maintained that her iron egg was the original, a Tamsui local legend claims a noodle stall owner named Huang Chang-nian invented the iron egg in the 1970s.
- (history) Iron eggs were popularized by shopkeeper Yang Bi-yun, who opened the first store selling the product in Tamsui in 1980.
- (about) The recooking and drying process eventually resulted in shrunken eggs that were dark, flavourful, and chewy, which were extremely popular with the locals.

- (inspiration) Iron eggs can only be created by the use of "chicken, pigeon or quail eggs" but not from duck eggs.
- (about) The popularity of iron eggs has risen and they can be found in other regions besides Taiwan, such as in Africa and the Middle East.

### Taiwanese beef noodle soup

Taiwanese beef noodle soup is a beef noodle soup dish that originated in Taiwan.

- From: Taiwan
- Origin (Wikidata): Taiwan

Fun facts:

- (culture) Taiwanese beef noodle soup is sometimes considered a national dish in Taiwan, and every year Taipei holds a Beef Noodle Festival, where various chefs and restaurants compete for the title of the "best beef noodles in Taiwan".
- (about) It is believed that the popularity of this noodle soup broke the beef-eating taboo and also paved the way to Taiwan's acceptance of American foods that utilized beef (such as hamburgers).
- (origin) Due to influences from the influx of immigrants from mainland China in the early 1900s, the Taiwanese version of beef noodle soup is now one of the most popular dishes in Taiwan.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) The Taiwanese traditionally had an aversion to the consumption of beef even into the mid-1970s because cattle were valuable beasts of burden, so originally the dish was only eaten by waishengren.
- (about) Taiwanese beef noodle soup became more accepted over time and is now one of the most famous dishes in Taiwanese cuisine.
- (about) The tomato variation is popular in Taiwan and features chunks of tomatoes in a rich red-coloured tomato broth with or without soy sauce.
