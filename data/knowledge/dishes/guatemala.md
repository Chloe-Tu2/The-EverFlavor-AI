### Fiambre

Fiambre is a traditional Guatemalan salad that is prepared and eaten yearly to celebrate All Saints Day, November 1, and All Souls' Day, November 2.

- From: Guatemala
- Origin (Wikidata): Guatemala

Fun facts:

- (culture) Fiambre started out from the tradition in Guatemala of taking dead family members their favorite dishes to the cemeteries for the Day of the Dead.
- (origin) Fiambre comes from Guatemala.
- (about) These days are also known as the Day of the Dead especially in Mexico.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) This dish varies from family to family, recipes traditionally passed on to younger generations.
- (culture) As all different families brought food to the celebrations, they became mixed, eventually mixing them together to this all-encompassing salad.

### Gallo en chicha

Gallo en chicha is a traditional chicken dish in Salvadoran cuisine, also eaten elsewhere in Central America, such as in Guatemala.

- From: Guatemala
- Ingredients (Wikidata): chicken as food

Fun facts:

- (culture) Gallo en chicha is somewhat similar to coq au vin, but like much of Salvadoran cuisine is a blend of European influences and the Salvadoran ingredients and cooking traditions.
- (culture) Because cooking this dish is complicated and time-consuming, it is usually prepared for special occasions and celebrations, such as holidays or the birth of a child.

### Kak'ik

Kak'ik is a soup made from a type of turkey called "chompipe" and is typical of Guatemalan cuisine.

- From: Guatemala
- Origin (Wikidata): Guatemala
- Ingredients (Wikidata): turkey leg

Fun facts:

- (about) In 2007, it was declared part of the "Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Nation".
- (origin) Kak'ik comes from Guatemala.
- (culture) Although there are regional variations, the traditional recipe of the Las Verapaces region includes chompipe, garlic, onion, peppermint, coriander and Eryngium foetidum (zamat) leaves.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Its red color can be associated with the blood that was used in pre-Columbian ritual sacrifices.

### Rellenitos de plátano

Rellenitos de plátano is a dish of sweet mashed plantains stuffed with a mixture of refried beans, chocolate, and cinnamon.

- From: Guatemala

Fun facts:

- (about) Rellenitos de plátano is a common and very popular dessert in the cuisine of Guatemala.
- (about) Rellenitos de plátano utilizes two of the most prevalent foods in the Latin American culture, black beans, known as frijoles negros in Spanish, and ripe plantains or plátanos.
- (about) The suffix ito in Spanish is known as a diminutive.
