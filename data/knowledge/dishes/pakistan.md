### Bannu pulao

Bannu pulao or Bannu beef pulao, also called Banuse pulao, is a traditional mixed rice dish from the Bannu district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan.

- From: Pakistan
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice, meat, spice

Fun facts:

- (about) Bannu pulao enjoys widespread popularity both within Pakistan and among countries where the Pakistani diaspora is present, particularly in the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia.

### Lassi

Lassi is a yogurt–based beverage with a smoothie-like consistency.

- From: Pakistan
- Origin (Wikidata): India
- Ingredients (Wikidata): yogurt, dahi

Fun facts:

- (history) The word 'lassi' for the drink is a borrowing from Hindi, first attested in 1882; its ultimate origin is unclear, and has been suggested as derived ultimately from Sanskrit lasya (stickiness) or rasah (juice).
- (origin) In Punjab, where the drink originated, the yogurt is traditionally made from water buffalo milk.
- (culture) Lassi is traditionally served in a clay cup known as kulhar.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) In the 21st century, lassi is commonly consumed in many world regions.
- (origin) Lassi comes from India.

### Black tea

Black tea (also literally translated as red tea from various East Asian languages) is a type of tea that is more oxidized than oolong, yellow, white, and green teas.

- From: Pakistan
- Origin (Wikidata): China
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Theaflavin-3,3'-digallate

Fun facts:

- (origin) First originating in China, the beverage's name there is hong cha due to the colour of the oxidized leaves when processed appropriately.
- (history) For this reason, it has long been an article of trade, and compressed bricks of black tea even served as a form of de facto currency in Mongolia, Tibet, and Siberia well into the 19th century.
- (origin) Black tea comes from China.

### Butter tea

Butter tea is a drink of the people in the Himalayan regions of Nepal, Bhutan, India, Pakistan especially in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Gilgit-Baltistan, Afghanistan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Xinjiang, Tibet, and western regions of modern-day China and Central Asia.

- From: Pakistan
- Origin (Wikidata): India
- Ingredients (Wikidata): tea, table salt, yak butter

Fun facts:

- (culture) Traditionally, it is made from tea leaves, yak butter, water, tsampa (roasted barley flour) and salt, although butter made from cow's milk is increasingly used, given its wider availability and lower cost.
- (origin) Butter tea comes from India.
