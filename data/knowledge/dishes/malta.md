### Imqaret

Imqaret are traditional Maltese sweets made with pastry and a filling of dates.

- From: Malta

Fun facts:

- (origin) Imqaret are of Arabic origin, introduced during the period of the Arab invasion of the island between 870 AD and the 11th century, while a similar sweet named makroudh or maqrud or makroud exists across the sea in Tunisia, which is also popular across Algeria and Morocco with the names makrout, maqrout, mqaret.
- (about) They are very popular in Malta and they are sold in street markets, as well as in village feasts, in some cases served with ice-cream.

### Stuffat tal-Fenek

Stuffat tal-Fenek is a type of rabbit stew in Maltese cuisine.

- From: Malta
- Origin (Wikidata): Malta

Fun facts:

- (about) Stuffat tal-Fenek is sometimes served in two courses by pouring the sauce over pasta as a first course and serving the rabbit and vegetables as a main course.
- (origin) The dish may have originated as a form of symbolic resistance to the hunting restrictions imposed by the Knights of St John.
- (history) The dish gained in popularity after the lifting of restrictions in the late 18th century (and by which time the indigenous breed, Tax-Xiber, had multiplied and prices dropped) and after local domestication of rabbits, a practice which may have been imported from France.

### Kinnie

Kinnie is a Maltese bittersweet carbonated soft drink brewed from bitter oranges and extracts of wormwood.

- From: Malta
- Origin (Wikidata): Malta
- Since: 1952-

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) Kinnie was invented by Simonds Farsons Cisk's managing director Anthony Miceli Farrugia and was first produced in 1952 as an alternative to the cola drinks that proliferated in post-war Europe, specifically Coca-Cola.
- (origin) Kinnie was first introduced in 1952 by the brewery Simonds Farsons Cisk, and continues to be produced by Farsons in Mrieħel, Birkirkara, Malta.
- (history) In 1975, it became the soft drink of the year of the French Comité International d'Action Gastronomique et Touristique.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) A Diet version for Kinnie appeared in 1984.
- (history) Kinnie dates from 1952-.
- (about) In 2007, a new low calorie version of Kinnie called Kinnie Zest was made available.
- (about) Simonds started selling Kinnie Vita in 2014, which is sweetened using a blend of sugar and stevia leaf extract.
- (about) In April 2021, Kinnie launched an alcoholic version of the drink called 'Kinnie Spritz'.
- (origin) Kinnie comes from Malta.

### Kusksu

Kusksu is a traditional Maltese soup made primarily from seasonal broad beans, small pasta beads, and fresh ġbejniet.

- From: Malta
- Origin (Wikidata): Malta
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Broad Bean, Ġbejna

Fun facts:

- (origin) Given its close resemblance to couscous, it is likely that kusksu originated during Malta's Arab occupation.
- (history) A reliable source dating back to the 18th century makes reference to peppercorn-shaped pasta, suggesting that kusksu pasta was a staple in the Maltese diet.
- (origin) Kusksu comes from Malta.
