### Bosintang

Bosintang or tan'gogikuk is a Korean soup (guk) that uses dog meat as its primary ingredient.

- From: South Korea
- Origin (Wikidata): Korea
- Ingredients (Wikidata): dog meat

Fun facts:

- (history) An 1849 book Dongguksesigi contains a recipe for bosintang that includes boiled dog and green onion.
- (about) Bosintang is also consumed in North Korea, where it is known as dangogiguk ("sweet meat soup") and was served at dinner parties hosted by Kim Jong Il before he became leader of the country.
- (origin) Bosintang comes from Korea.

### Budae-jjigae

Budae-jjigae is a type of spicy jjigae (Korean stew) from South Korea that is made with a variety of ingredients, often canned or processed.

- From: South Korea
- Origin (Wikidata): South Korea
- Since: 1960-
- Named after: United States Forces Korea
- Ingredients (Wikidata): sausage, Kimchi, ham, Spam, gochujang, baked beans

Fun facts:

- (history) A prominent ingredient of the dish, Spam, was only made legally available for sale in 1987, around the time that South Korea democratized.
- (origin) Gyeonggi Province's city of Uijeongbu, which claims to have first made the dish, has a "Uijeongbu Budae-jjigae Street" with a high concentration of specialty restaurants.
- (inspiration) Budae-jjigae is named after United States Forces Korea.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Budae-jjigae dates from 1960-.
- (about) Budae-jjigae is now a popular anju (accompaniment to alcoholic drinks) and a comfort food cooked in a large pot shared by a group of people.
- (about) Although the dish came from conditions of poverty, it has remained consistently popular, even during and after South Korea's rapid economic growth.
- (origin) Budae-jjigae comes from South Korea.

### Bungeo-ppang

Bungeo-ppang is a fish-shaped pastry stuffed with sweetened red bean paste, which originated from the Japanese taiyaki.

- From: South Korea
- Origin (Wikidata): Korea
- Ingredients (Wikidata): batter

Fun facts:

- (culture) One of South Korea's most popular winter street foods, the snack is often sold at street stalls, grilled on an appliance similar to a waffle iron but with a fish-shaped mold.
- (origin) Bungeo-ppang comes from Korea.
- (culture) Red bean paste is the standard filling but many bungeo-ppang sold as street food are filled with pastry cream (called "choux-bung" as the cream is called "choux-cream" in South Korea), sweet potato, pizza toppings, chocolate, kimchi and others.

- (culture) Originally served at royal court banquets, galbi-tang has evolved into a popular dish frequently offered at modern weddings and celebratory events, as well as a well-known nourishing soup enjoyed during the cold winter months.
