### Haleem

Haleem or halim is a type of stew that is widely consumed in West Asia, Central Asia and South Asia.

- From: Mauritius
- Ingredients (Wikidata): meat, wheat, lentil

Fun facts:

- (history) During the Mughal Empire (1526–1857), which further strengthened Persianate culture in the Indian subcontinent, the dish continued to evolve.
- (origin) The dish now known as haleem is believed to have been introduced to the Indian subcontinent through Arab traders, missionaries, and military expeditions, particularly between the 12th and 16th centuries.
- (about) Popular variations of haleem include halim (in Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan, India, Mauritius, Pakistan and Bangladesh); keşkek (in Turkey, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan and Northern Iraq); harisa (in the Arab world and Armenia); and Hyderabadi haleem (in India).

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) One of the key phases in this transmission was during the Delhi Sultanate period (1206–1526), when Muslim rule was established across large parts of northern India.
- (about) Haleem is generally considered to have evolved from the Arab dish known as Harees (also spelled Harisa or Jareesh).
- (culture) Haleem is traditionally cooked in large, wood-fired cauldrons.
- (about) Haleem is also very popular in Bangladesh, especially during the holy month of Ramadan, when it is a staple dish.
- (about) One common Bangladeshi version of Halim is called "Shahi Halim"; it is mostly popular in the central part of the country.
- (history) A 10th century cookbook, Kitāb al-Ṭabīkh (Book of Dishes) by Ibn Sayaar Al Warraq, has numerous versions of this porridge.
- (culture) Harees was traditionally made with meat, wheat, and sometimes lentils, slow-cooked until soft and porridge-like in consistency.
- (origin) Arab and Persian cultural influences, including culinary practices, were introduced by the ruling elite, soldiers, and Sufi saints.

### Rougaille

Rougaille, also written as rougail (and sometimes rougay), is a spiced tomato sauce common in the cuisines of Mauritius, Réunion, Seychelles, and other parts of the Francophone Indian Ocean and West Indies.

- From: Mauritius
- Origin (Wikidata): France, Mauritius

Fun facts:

- (culture) Rougaille is used in main dishes, snacks and street foods, and as a dipping or table sauce.
- (origin) Rougaille comes from France, Mauritius.
