### Githeri

Githeri (Gĩtheri), also called muthere or mutheri, mûthekia-ngûkû is a traditional Kenyan meal consisting of maize kernels and legumes (primarily beans) mixed and boiled together.

- From: Kenya
- Origin (Wikidata): Kenya
- Ingredients (Wikidata): maize, bean

Fun facts:

- (history) This popular dish has been the main meal served to students in Kenya since the 1920s.
- (about) Githeri is also popular in other parts of the country, such as with the Kamba people of the Eastern Province, and communities in Western and Rift Valley parts of Kenya.
- (culture) Githeri is widespread and is also a traditional dish from North Africa to South Africa.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Lately, there has been a rise in the popularity of githeri due to the health benefits associated with this plant-based dish.
- (about) There was a spike in popularity in 2017 when a voter, Martin Kamotho, was spotted eating githeri while waiting to vote and was labelled as githeri man.
- (origin) Githeri comes from Kenya.

### Nyama choma

Nyama Choma is a specialty of grilled or barbecued goat meat or cattle meat.

- From: Kenya
- Origin (Wikidata): Kenya, Tanzania
- Ingredients (Wikidata): mutton

Fun facts:

- (culture) Roasted meat is very popular in Tanzania and Kenya, where it is considered the national dish.

### Arrowroot

Arrowroot is a starch obtained from the rhizomes (rootstock) of several tropical plants, traditionally in particular Maranta arundinacea, but also Florida arrowroot (Zamia integrifolia), as well as tapioca from cassava (Manihot esculenta), which is often labeled arrowroot.

- From: Kenya
- Ingredients (Wikidata): arrowroot

Fun facts:

- (about) M. arundinacea is called ପାଳୁଅ in Odisha, India, and uraró/araró in the Philippines, where it is the base starch of a popular cookie.
- (about) Not to be confused with arrowroot.

### Mursik

Mursik is a traditional fermented milk variant of the Kalenjin people of Kenya.

- From: Kenya
- Origin (Wikidata): Kenya
- Ingredients (Wikidata): milk

Fun facts:

- (about) Mursik can be made from cow or goat milk and is fermented in a specially made calabash gourd locally known as a sotet.
- (culture) At the end of the negotiations, mursik is served and drunk together by those present as a symbol of agreement and unity, it is considered crucial to the process.
- (origin) Mursik comes from Kenya.
