### Banitsa

Banitsa, also transliterated as banica and banitza, is a traditional pie made in Bulgaria.

- From: Bulgaria
- Origin (Wikidata): Bulgaria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): egg as food, phyllo

Fun facts:

- (about) Banitsa is also made in Budjak, where it is known as milina by Ukrainian Bulgarians; North Macedonia; and southeastern Serbia.
- (culture) Banitsa is considered a symbol of Bulgarian cuisine and traditions.
- (origin) Banitsa comes from Bulgaria.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) In southeastern Serbia, it may also be known as gibanica.
- (culture) Traditionally, Bulgarians prepare and serve banitsa on two holidays –Christmas and New Year's Eve.
- (about) Not to be confused with Banitsa.
- (culture) Traditionally in Bulgaria, lucky charms are put into the pie on certain occasions, particularly on New Year's Eve.
- (culture) These charms may be coins or small symbolic objects (e.g., a small piece of a dogwood branch with a bud, symbolizing health or longevity).
- (culture) These are usually small pieces of dogwood branch, which symbolize health and longevity.

### Shopska salad

Shopska salad is a cold salad popular throughout Southeastern Europe.

- From: Bulgaria
- Origin (Wikidata): Bulgaria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): cheese, Cucumis sativus, tomato, onion, sirene

Fun facts:

- (history) Shopska salad can be found in one of the first state-approved cookbooks from 1956 (Sbornik recepti 1956, vol. 1, p. 50).
- (origin) The term "Shopska salad" first appeared in a Bulgarian cookbook from 1940, but it referred to a recipe for some kind of lyutenitsa.
- (culture) Shopska salad was approved as a national culinary symbol during the 1970s and 1980s.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Shopska salad is Bulgaria's most famous salad and national dish.
- (history) According to historical sources, in the last decades of the 19th century, Bulgarians had a vague idea of what a salad was.
- (about) Then salads were first adopted by the urban population and later recipes for them began to be published in cookbooks.
- (history) The Shopska salad is a variation on a Greek salad of cucumbers, tomatoes, and feta; Balkantourist in 1956 named their version Shopska salad.
- (origin) Despite the fact that the salad's name comes from the westernmost Bulgarian region called Shopluk, it first appeared at the Black Sea coast, in a resort near Varna, called Druzhba in the restaurant "Chernomorets".
- (history) The development and popularization of the salad is attributed to a leader in Bulgarian tourism Petar Doychev (1924–2019).
- (origin) Shopska salad comes from Bulgaria.
- (history) Tomatoes appeared in Southeastern Europe in the mid-1800s.
- (history) The import of vegetable seeds to Bulgaria was established only in the 1930s.

### Pelin wine

Pelin wine is made by mixing wine with Artemisia absinthium during fermentation, giving the wine a bitter, refreshing taste.

- From: Bulgaria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): wine, Artemisia absinthium

Fun facts:

- (origin) When the wine was first made, it was stored in amphorae and sealed using a sealant made from fir trees.
- (culture) During the Communist era, Pelin wine remained very popular, but most of the traditions associated with it were lost in the first decade after the that era passed.
- (about) This wine is popular in Eastern-Europe from the 1st of May until the summer.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (inspiration) Pelin wine differs from absinthe and pelinkovac, which are based on distilled alcohol rather than wine.
- (culture) In recent years, though, these traditions have been revived and the wine producers have started to make Pelin wine according to traditional recipes again.
