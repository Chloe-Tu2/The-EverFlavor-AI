### Alivenci

Alivenci, plural form of alivancă, is a traditional custard tart, from the cuisine of Moldavia made with cornmeal, cream cheese like urdă or telemea and smântână.

- From: Moldova
- Origin (Wikidata): Moldova
- Ingredients (Wikidata): milk

Fun facts:

- (origin) Alivenci comes from Moldova.
- (history) A form of cheesecake was very popular in Ancient Greece.

### Sfințișori

Sfințișori (Little saints) or mucenici moldovenești (Moldavian martyrs), are traditional pastries from Romania and Moldova made to commemorate on March 9 (or March 22, as per Julian calendar), the Christian feast of the 40 Martyrs of Sebaste, a traditional holiday in Romania and Moldova.

- From: Moldova

### Moldovan wine

Moldova has a well-established wine industry.

- From: Moldova

Fun facts:

- (history) After the phylloxera damage at the end of the 19th century, it was only in 1906 that the vineyards began to recover with grafted planting material.
- (about) With a production of around 2 million hectolitres of wine (as of 2018), it is the 11th largest European wine-producing country.
- (origin) The second half of the 19th century saw an intensive planting of newly introduced French varieties, such as Pinot blanc, Pinot noir, Pinot gris, Aligote, Cabernet Sauvignon, Sauvignon blanc, Gamay, Muscat blanc.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) In the 1980s Russia was suffering from alcoholism and ordered the destruction of vineyards; between 1985 and 1987, 30% were destroyed.
- (history) After the Treaty of Bucharest in 1812, when the region became a province of the Russian Empire, the wine industry flourished again.
- (history) In this period, the grape growers gained governmental support, and by 1837 the vineyard area in Bessarabia reached 14,000 hectares, with wine production of 12 million litres.
- (history) The re-establishment of Moldovan vineyards began in the 1950s, during Soviet rule.
- (about) In 2022 the majority of wines were exported, being sent to 75 countries, with 60% of wine produced being exported to European Union countries.
- (about) Moldovan wine was at this time that wines like Negru de Purcari and Romănești, which have made Moldova famous as a fine wine producer, began to be produced.
- (history) By 1914, Bessarabia had the biggest vineyard area in the Russian Empire.
- (history) Over 150,000 hectares were planted in 10 years, and by 1960 the total vineyard area had reached 220,000 hectares.
- (culture) The main varieties were the traditional ones: Rară neagră, Plavaie, Galbenă, Zghiharda, Batuta neagră, Fetească albă, Fetească neagră, Tămâioasă, Cabasia and many other local, Hungarian, Bulgarian, Greek, and Turkish varieties.

### Murături

Murături (Romanian for pickles) are vegetables or fruit pickled in brine, with added dill, oak leaves, celery and others for flavoring and preservation, as found in Romanian cuisine and Moldovan cuisine.

- From: Moldova
