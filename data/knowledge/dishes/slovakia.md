### Bryndzové halušky

Bryndzové halušky, Austrian German: Brimsennocken) is one of the national dishes in Slovakia.

- From: Slovakia
- Origin (Wikidata): Slovakia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): table salt, flour, Bryndza, corned beef, potato

Fun facts:

- (culture) There is an annual bryndzové halušky festival in Turecká that features an eating contest.
- (origin) Bryndzové halušky comes from Slovakia.
- (about) In the Kingdom of Hungary, two types of galuska were developed at the same time: the potato galuska of the mountains and the galuska of the plains, without potatoes.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) This meal consists of halušky (boiled lumps of potato dough similar in appearance to gnocchi) and bryndza (a soft sheep cheese), optionally sprinkled with cooked bits of smoked pork fat or bacon, and chives or spring onions. Žinčica is traditionally drunk with this meal.

### Lokša

### Nut roll

A nut roll is a pastry consisting of a sweet yeast dough (usually using milk) that is rolled out very thin, spread with a nut paste made from ground nuts and a sweetener like honey, then rolled up into a log shape.

- From: Slovakia

Fun facts:

- (origin) In the United States, "nut roll" is a more or less generic name for pastries of this type, no matter where they originate.
- (culture) Regional variations on nut rolls are part of weddings, for Easter and Christmas, as well as other celebrations and holidays.
- (about) Not to be confused with poppy seed roll.

### Strapačky

Strapačky (Slovak) or sztrapacska (Hungarian) is a dish popular in Slovakia and Hungary.

- From: Slovakia
- Origin (Wikidata): Slovakia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): halušky

Fun facts:

- (culture) Residents of Vanyarc, located in Hungary approximately 80 kilometers from Budapest, have held a Festival of Strapačky (Vanyarci Haluskafesztivál) every September for almost 40 years.
- (origin) Strapačky comes from Slovakia.
- (culture) Ethnic Slovaks from all over Hungary compete for prizes at the festival.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) In Hungary, sztrapacska is traditionally accompanied with juhtúró, the local equivalent to bryndza.
