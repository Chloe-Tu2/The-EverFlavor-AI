### Berkoukes (dish)

Berkoukes, also named berkoukech, aïch or avazine is a traditional North African dish, particularly popular in Algeria, Tunisia, and parts of Morocco.

- From: Algeria
- Ingredients (Wikidata): vegetable, spice, semolina

Fun facts:

- (history) Historical sources suggest that in the 12th century, North Africa had as many pasta varieties as couscous preparations, with berkoukes among them.
- (culture) The tradition of making pasta in North Africa dates back centuries.
- (culture) These traditional pastas were valued for being affordable, nutritious, and adaptable to different regional flavors.

### Dawamesc

Dawamesc is a cannabis edible found in Algeria and some other Arab countries, made of cannabis tops combined with: "sugar, orange juice, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom, nutmeg, musk, pistachios, and pine nuts." The edible played a role in popularizing cannabis in Europe, as it was this preparation of the drug which Dr. Jacques-Joseph Moreau observed during his travels in Northern Africa, and that he introduced to Paris' Club des Hashischins.

- Origin (Wikidata): Algeria

### Algerian wine

Algerian wine is wine cultivated and bottled in Algeria.

- From: Algeria

Fun facts:

- (history) Prior to the Algerian War of Independence (1954–1962), Algeria was the largest wine exporter in the world, accounting for nearly two-thirds of the total international wine trade.
- (origin) In 1935, appellations were introduced to protect regional production and ensure that Algerian wine was not being sold as a French product, which was often the case.
- (about) In 2019, Algeria produced approximately 500,000 hectoliters of wine, making it one of the largest wine-producing countries in Africa.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) From 1854 to 1915, wine production grew from 25K hectoliters to 10 million hectoliters.
- (history) During the late 19th century, Algeria's wine production peaked and it became the largest wine-producing country in the world, with an annual production of over 3 billion liters of wine.
- (history) When Algeria came under French rule in 1830 vineyards were replanted to serve the needs of the local pieds-noir.
- (history) When the phylloxera epidemic destroyed the French vineyards in the mid-19th century, Algerian wine exports into France filled the void.
- (history) Starting in the 1880s, the price of French wines started to decrease, leading the wine industry to lobby in favor of restricted imports and higher quality standards.
- (history) The high point of the Algerian wine industry came in the late 1930s when over 4,000 square kilometres (1,500 sq mi) was producing more 2,100 megalitres (550,000,000 US gal) of wine.
- (history) By the 1950s, together with Tunisia and Morocco, Algerian wine accounted for nearly two-thirds of the wine that was internationally traded.
- (history) The dominant grape variety in Algeria at this time was Carignan, which only overtook Aramon in southern France in the 1960s.
- (history) By 1961, Algeria was the fourth producer of wine worldwide after France, Italy and Spain.
- (history) By the time of Algerian independence in 1962, over a dozen areas were granted Vin Délimité de Qualité Superieure (VDQS) status by the French.
