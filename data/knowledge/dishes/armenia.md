### Ghapama

Ghapama is an Armenian stuffed pumpkin dish, usually served as a dessert.

- From: Armenia
- Origin (Wikidata): Armenia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice, pumpkin

Fun facts:

- (about) Ghapama is prepared by removing the inside of the pumpkin and stuffing it with cooked rice, butter, honey, dried fruits, nuts, and meat, to be baked.
- (culture) Ghapama is usually served during autumn time in Armenia to celebrate the harvest during the season.
- (origin) Ghapama comes from Armenia.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Ghapama is also served at other major celebrations, such as Christmas, New Year, Easter, and weddings.
- (origin) Historically, crops such as pumpkins, potatoes, peppers, and tomatoes, were brought to Armenia in the 19th century from the "New World".
- (culture) The crops were mixed with traditional Armenian dishes and were then "naturalized" to Armenian cuisine according to writers Irina Petrosian and David Underwood.
- (culture) The song is commonly played at weddings was popularized by pop singer Harout Pamboukjian and by a Russian spin-off of My Big Fat Greek Wedding named My Big Armenian Wedding.

### Chechil

Chechil (also chechili) is a brined string cheese primarily made from skimmed cow's milk, though it can also be produced from mixtures of cow, sheep, and goat milk.

- From: Armenia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): goat milk, sheep milk, cow's milk

Fun facts:

- (about) The resulting curd was salted, kneaded in salted water, and formed into large circles or bundles of thin strands, known as chetchil or chechil.
- (origin) Armenian refugees who settled in Syria after the 1915 Armenian genocide introduced the cheese there.
- (about) The cheese is popular in Armenia and Georgia.
