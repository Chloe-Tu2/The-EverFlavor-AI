### Buuz

Buuz (; Mongolian: ᠪᠤᠤᠵᠠ [pʊ́ːts]; Buryat: Бууза ᠪᠤᠤᠽᠠ [ˈbʊː(d)z]) are a type of Mongolian steamed dumpling filled with meat.

- From: Mongolia
- Origin (Wikidata): Russia, Mongolia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): meat, table salt, wheat flour, onion, black pepper

Fun facts:

- (culture) An example of authentic Mongolian and Buryatian cuisine, the dish is traditionally eaten at home during Tsagaan Sar, the Lunar New Year.
- (origin) Buuz comes from Russia, Mongolia.
- (about) Niislel salad, a variant of Olivier potato salad, is particularly popular, being almost ubiquitous among banquets and formal meals in Mongolian households.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) They are eaten in great quantities throughout the year but especially during the Mongolian New Year celebrations, which usually fall in February.

### Khuushuur

Khuushuur is a traditional Mongolian fried meat pastry.

- From: Mongolia
- Origin (Wikidata): Mongolia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): wheat flour

Fun facts:

- (culture) Khuushuur is commonly associated with Naadam, Mongolia’s summer festival.
- (origin) Khuushuur comes from Mongolia.
- (origin) Wheat was not traditionally grown in Mongolia due to the nomadic lifestyle, but the influence of Chinese cuisine introduced dumpling-like preparations that became part of Mongolian food culture.

### Arkhi

Arkhi is a liquor made from airag, fermented milk brandy, or isgelen tarag which then gets distilled.

- From: Mongolia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): kumis, khormoog, yak milk

Fun facts:

- (history) The Mongols adopted the distillation technology from Arab and Islamic producers of arak, a grape-based spirit, substituting their own local fermented ingredients to distill sometime in the 13th century.
- (culture) Isgelen tarag often uses the milk of a mare, donkeys, sheep, cows, the yak, camels (specifically, khoormog) or of reindeer, depending on local traditions or availability.

### Suutei tsai

Suutei tsai (Mongolian: сүүтэй цай (Cyrillic) ᠰᠦᠨ ᠲᠡᠢᠴᠠᠢ(Mongolian script) [ˈsuːtʰeː ˈt͡sʰɛː]) is a traditional Mongolian beverage made from tea, milk, millet and salt.

- From: Mongolia
- Origin (Wikidata): Mongolia
- Ingredients (Wikidata): tea

Fun facts:

- (about) The drink is also known as süütei tsai, tsutai tsai, salt tea, or Mongolian salty tea.
- (origin) Suutei tsai comes from Mongolia.
- (culture) An old tradition among many Mongols was to not drink water straight.
