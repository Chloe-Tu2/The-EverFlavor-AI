### Hainanese curry rice

Hainanese curry rice is a Singaporean dish consisting of steamed white rice smothered with curries and gravy, characteristically accompanied by curry chicken, pork chop, chap chye (braised cabbage) and kong bak (braised pork).

- From: Singapore
- Origin (Wikidata): Singapore
- Ingredients (Wikidata): curry

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) It originates in Singaporean cuisine and is not thought of as part of the cuisine of Hainan, China although it was created by Hainanese people.
- (history) Loo's, one of the most well-known and popular Hainanese curry rice vendors in Singapore, started operations in 1946 and is situated in Tiong Bahru Market.
- (origin) Hainanese curry rice comes from Singapore.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (inspiration) Pork chop was adapted from British cuisine and the rest of the ingredients, such as curry chicken, babi pongteh and chap chye, were from Peranakan cuisine.
- (about) These were adapted for Hainanese curry rice.

### Katong laksa

Katong laksa is a variant of laksa lemak inspired by the Peranakans who live in the precinct of Katong in Singapore.

- From: Singapore
- Origin (Wikidata): Singapore

Fun facts:

- (history) Katong laksa was first popularised by brothers Ng Juat Swee, also known as "Janggut", and Ng Chwee Seng, who started hawking Peranakan-style laksa on the streets in the 1940s but moved to a stall in the Hock Tong Hin coffee shop at 49 East Coast Road in 1950.
- (about) Katong laksa was so named due to its origins in the area.
- (origin) Katong laksa comes from Singapore.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) In 1963, the stall was registered under the name "Marine Parade Laksa".

### Singapore-style noodles

Singapore-style noodles is a dish of stir-fried cooked rice vermicelli, curry powder, vegetables, scrambled eggs and meat – most commonly char siu pork, prawn, and/or chicken.

- From: Singapore
- Named after: Singapore
- Ingredients (Wikidata): rice vermicelli

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) Singapore-style noodles is named after Singapore.
- (origin) The dish dates back to just after World War II, having been developed by Cantonese chefs who wanted ways to use curry powder – which had been introduced through the British colonies.

### Yam ring

Yam ring, also known as yam basket, taro basket or prosperity basket, is a Singaporean dish consisting of a deep-fried ring of mashed taro filled with separately stir-fried ingredients.

- From: Singapore
- Origin (Wikidata): Singapore

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) According to the legend, Chef Hooi invented the dish in 1958 to impress the vegetarian nuns who had brought up his orphaned wife-to-be, Leong Ah Lin.
- (history) Yam ring is commonly credited to Hooi Kok Wai, the founder of the Dragon Phoenix Restaurant in Singapore and one of the "Four Heavenly Kings of Cantonese Cuisine" in the 1960s, chefs who cooked Cantonese–style dishes with local Singaporean ingredients in Singapore.
- (about) Lai Wah Restaurant, set up by two other Heavenly Kings, also claims credit for inventing the dish.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Yam ring comes from Singapore.

### Singapore sling

The Singapore sling is a gin-based sling cocktail from Singapore.

- From: Singapore
- Origin (Wikidata): Singapore
- Ingredients (Wikidata): Cointreau, Bénédictine, Angostura bitters, gin, maraschino cherry, grenadine, pineapple juice, highball glass, lime juice, pineapple

Fun facts:

- (history) This long drink was reputed to have been developed in 1915 by Ngiam Tong Boon, a bartender at the Long Bar in Raffles Hotel, Singapore.
- (origin) The Long Bar at Raffles Hotel sells 800-1200 Singapore slings every day. 70% of the total revenue of the bar comes from the sling, which earns the bar S$15 million in annual sales.
- (inspiration) The hotel's recipe was recreated based on a 1936 note by a visitor.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (origin) Singapore sling was first made in Raffles Hotel.
- (history) The drink was created sometime between 1899 and 1915 at Raffles Hotel.
- (history) On the other hand, David Wondrich of Esquire claimed that the drink was created in the 1890s and wasn't related to the Raffles until the 1920s.
- (origin) Singapore sling comes from Singapore.
- (history) This is because the original recipe was lost after the 1930s when the hotel stopped serving the drink.
