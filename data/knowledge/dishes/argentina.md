### Asado

- From: Argentina

- Ingredients (Wikidata): chorizo, llama, blood sausage, pork, beef, lamb meat

Fun facts:

- (origin) Asado comes from Argentina.

### Chipa

Chipa is a type of small, baked, cheese-flavored roll, a popular snack and breakfast food in Paraguay.

- From: Argentina
- Origin (Wikidata): Paraguay

Fun facts:

- (history) This province, dependent on the Viceroyalty of Peru, covered the regions of Paraguay, Argentina, Uruguay, and parts of Bolivia, Brazil and Chile (between 1604 and 1617).
- (about) Schmidl was in charge of noting in the logbook of the Spanish ship in which the expedition led by Juan de Ayolas arrived, which would arrive in Asunción later, thus giving rise to the first encounter between Spaniards and the Cario-Guarani people.
- (origin) For these reasons, the Paraguayan culture that characterizes Asunción remained strong in this area, and in turn spread to areas where the cattle were later introduced, with the founding of Corrientes in 1588, the oldest city in the northeast of Argentina.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Then this region became part of the ephemeral Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata (1776–1810).
- (history) The recipe has existed since the 18th century and its origins are inexpensive and often sold from streetside stands and on buses by vendors carrying a large basket with the warm chipa wrapped in a cloth.
- (history) When the Jesuits were expelled in 1767, the natives returned to their natural habitat (the Atlantic jungle) and they never visited Asunción and its area of influence to educate or teach, proof of this is the extinction of Jesuit ceramics and not the Franciscan that is still alive in Itá, Areguá and Tobatí.
- (about) The first antecedents of Spanish and Cario-Guaraní syncretism took place at the time of the foundation of Asunción and surroundings, where the Franciscan reductions of Altos, Atyrá, Guarambaré, Itá, etc. were later founded.
- (history) Since 1617, the Paraguaria Province was dismembered to the Governorate of the Río de la Plata and the Governorate of Paraguay, thus remaining under the jurisdiction of the latter.
- (history) This was due to the introduction of cattle in 1556 in Asunción, so thanks to these animals the new ingredients were finally obtained such as beef, milk, eggs, cheese, etc.
- (about) Before being known as chipa, there was a menu that was already part of the varieties of bread that the Cario-Guarani natives had in the early days of the conquest.
- (about) Back then, the food before the chipa was known as "mbujape", which translated from Guarani means "bread".
- (origin) Chipa comes from Paraguay.
- (about) Not to be confused with Cheese bread, Cuñapé.

### Locro

Locro or lojro (from the Quechua ruqru) is a hearty thick squash or potato stew, associated with Native Andean civilizations, and popular along the Andes mountain range.

- From: Argentina
- Ingredients (Wikidata): meat

Fun facts:

- (culture) Locro is one of the national dishes of Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Chile, Paraguay, Argentina and Southwestern Colombia.
- (about) The first version of Locro was made of potatoes, chili peppers, salt, water and Chuño, which allowed the populations to subsist despite their otherwise low-protein-containing diet.
- (culture) Locro is among the most important main foods representing Argentinan gastronomy, ranking as third among 28 different foods in a study regarding traditional Argentinan gastronomy.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Not to be confused with Locrus.

### Picada (Rioplatense cuisine)

A picada is a typical dish of Argentine and Uruguayan cuisine usually served as a starter, although sometimes as a main course.

- From: Argentina
- Origin (Wikidata): Uruguay, Argentina

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) In the 1940s Buenos Aires' restaurants, a copetín was a tapas-inspired started served in a metal "triolet" plate, that included olives, potato chips, peanuts and palitos salados (flour-based snack sticks).
- (about) One of the most popular dishes in the Rio de la Plata cuisine, picadas are a social event that involves gathering with family or friends.
- (culture) Picada is considered a culinary heritage of the massive Spanish and Italian immigration to the Río de la Plata region —encompassing both Argentina and Uruguay—, introducing the tradition of tapas and antipasto, respectively.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Although it was traditionally served as a starter, in recent years the picada has gained popularity as a main dish.
- (culture) In Uruguay, picadas made with different ingredients are served in restaurants, mainly in the traditional and oldest bars in Montevideo.
- (origin) Picada comes from Uruguay, Argentina.

### Pollo al disco

Pollo al disco is a hearty Argentine dish consisting of chicken and various vegetables cooked as a stew in an uncovered deep pan over an open fire. "Disco" refers to the pan used to cook the chicken, which is usually around 24 inches in diameter and at least 6 inches deep.

- From: Argentina
- Origin (Wikidata): Argentina

Fun facts:

- (origin) It is unknown where and when pollo al disco originated, but it is thought that the first pollo al disco was cooked using a disused plow disk in a rural area of Buenos Aires province.
- (about) Pollo al disco remains a popular meal for parties and other gatherings.
- (origin) Pollo al disco comes from Argentina.

### Revuelto Gramajo

Revuelto Gramajo is a common hash dish in Argentine cuisine consisting of fried julienned potatoes, ham and eggs.

- From: Argentina
- Origin (Wikidata): Argentina

### Sorrentinos

Sorrentinos are a type of Argentine ravioli, but larger, more circular and originally wrapped without fluted edge.

- From: Argentina
- Origin (Wikidata): Argentina
- Since: 1958-

Fun facts:

- (inspiration) There is a slightly different version of the later story, in which a chef from Mar del Plata made the first sorrentinos in a Buenos Aires' restaurant whose name is also Sorrento.
- (about) It is believed that sorrentinos are a local variant of the Italian ravioli capresi, whose dough is instead elaborated with flour, water and olive oil, while the filling is made with caciotta cheese, flavoured with oregano.
- (origin) Most sources point to an Italian immigrant from Sorrento, Rosalía Pérsico or his son Cayetano Pérsico, who created this pasta while working in a famous trattoria of Mar del Plata, while other sources state that they originated in another restaurant in Mar del Plata called Sorrento.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (about) Argentino "Chiche" Véspoli, another immigrant from Sorrento was the owner of the first restaurant to serve up the dish, and recognized as such by Sorrento's authorities and the Sorrento's chapter of Lions Club.
- (history) Sorrentinos dates from 1958-.
- (origin) Sorrentinos was first made in Mar del Plata.
- (origin) Sorrentinos comes from Argentina.
