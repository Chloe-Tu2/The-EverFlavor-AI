### Flaouna

Flaouna is a cheese-filled baked goods from Cyprus, also consumed in parts of Greece.

- From: Cyprus
- Origin (Wikidata): Cyprus
- Ingredients (Wikidata): drinking water, milk, cheese, table salt, raisin, vanilla, flour, nutmeg, mastic, mahleb

Fun facts:

- (about) The Guinness World Records holds a record for the largest flaouna ever made.
- (culture) Flaounes are traditionally prepared for Easter.
- (origin) Flaouna comes from Cyprus.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) Flaounes are closely tied to Easter customs and are traditionally prepared in large batches by families in the days leading up to the celebration.
- (culture) Flaounes are traditionally served in Cyprus, parts of Greece (especially Arcadia) and more widely in the Greek diaspora as a celebratory food for the breaking of the Lenten fast, being prepared on Great and Holy Friday for consumption on Easter Sunday.
- (culture) Creating the flaounes can often be a family tradition shared with multiple generations.

### Brandy sour

The brandy sour is a mixed alcoholic cocktail considered the unofficial national cocktail of Cyprus.

- From: Cyprus

Fun facts:

- (about) The cocktail is said to have been developed by barman Mr Sourmelis at the Forest Park Hotel, in the hill-resort of Plátres, for the young King Farouk of Egypt, who often stayed at the hotel during his frequent visits to the island.
- (inspiration) The story lacks credibility, because no matter how lavish and extravagant the lifestyle of King Farouk was, he did not drink alcohol.

### KEO (beer)

KEO is a Cypriot beer.

- From: Cyprus

Fun facts:

- (history) During British colonial rule and following the successful introduction of industrial beer production in 1937, the KEO company launched their beer product in 1951.
- (about) In 2010, KEO held a 32.4% share of the island's beer market.
- (history) The beer won the 1987 brewing industry world bottled lager competition gold medal.
