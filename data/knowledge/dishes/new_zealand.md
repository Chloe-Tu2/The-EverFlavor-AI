### Chocolate fish

A chocolate fish or choccy fish is a traditional confectionery item in New Zealand.

- From: New Zealand
- Origin (Wikidata): New Zealand

Fun facts:

- (history) Chocolate fish have been made since at least 1903, though early varieties may not have included marshmallow.
- (culture) Variants of the traditional item exist; a common version is made of solid orange-flavoured milk chocolate.
- (about) In 2019, the chocolate fish was added to the Cadbury Favourites box.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Marshmallow-based chocolate fish were being produced in Dunedin in 1937.
- (history) For a short period in the late 1990s to early 2000s, Tip Top sold a chocolate fish ice-cream.
- (history) In 1970, Ron Barclay, Member of Parliament for New Plymouth, asked a question in Parliament about the price of chocolate fish.
- (history) In 1996 a "Great New Zealand Cadbury Chocolate Fish Day" was created in May to support the Child Safety Foundation of New Zealand and the Cystic Fibrosis Association of New Zealand.
- (about) Cadbury stopped producing 20g chocolate fish in 2023, stating that they had become less popular with consumers.
- (origin) Chocolate fish comes from New Zealand.

### Street food

Street food is food sold by a hawker or vendor on a street or at another public place, such as a market, fair, or park.

- From: New Zealand

Fun facts:

- (culture) Some street foods are regional, but many have spread beyond their regions of origin.
- (culture) Most street foods are classified as both finger food and fast food, and are generally cheaper than restaurant meals.
- (culture) The types of street food vary between regions and cultures in different countries around the world.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (culture) According to a 2007 study from the Food and Agriculture Organization, 2.5 billion people eat street food every day.
- (culture) While some cultures consider it to be rude to walk on the street while eating, a majority of middle- to high-income consumers rely on the quick access and affordability of street food for daily nutrition and job opportunities, particularly in developing countries.
- (culture) Today governments and other organizations are increasingly concerned with both the socioeconomic importance of street food and its associated risks.

### Kānga pirau

Kānga pirau (which translates literally from Māori as rotten corn), is a fermented maize (corn) porridge dish which is considered a delicacy by many Māori people of New Zealand.

- From: New Zealand
- Origin (Wikidata): New Zealand

Fun facts:

- (history) The dish dates back to at least the 19th century.
- (origin) Kānga pirau comes from New Zealand.

### Kānga waru

Kānga waru (waru means 'to scrape') is a type of pudding from New Zealand.

- From: New Zealand

Fun facts:

- (culture) Scrapping (waru) was one of the common preparation methods used by Polynesians for traditional puddings.
- (origin) Corn (borrowed from English as kānga) was introduced to New Zealand by early Europeans and was adopted by Māori as a food crop.
- (origin) The dessert originates from the Māori people and is closely associated with Māori cuisine.

### Pāua

Pāua is the Māori name given to four New Zealand species of large edible sea snails, marine gastropod molluscs which belong to the family Haliotidae (in which there is only one genus, Haliotis).

- From: New Zealand

Fun facts:

- (about) Pāua is known in Australia and the United States as abalone, and in the United Kingdom as ormer shells.
- (history) The industry was started in New Zealand in the 1980s, and to date there are fourteen pāua farms operating throughout New Zealand, from Whangārei to Stewart Island.
- (about) The first farms consisted of small-scale backyard and shed farms in which techniques and processes that grew good-quality pāua were developed; these are now being used on a commercial scale in large pāua farms.

More fun facts (when the user asks for more):

- (history) Pāua shells make up the decoration on Xena's first chakram in the hit TV series Xena: Warrior Princess.
- (about) In New Zealand, these are known as pāua, which (as is the case with nearly all Māori words) is both singular and plural.
- (about) In 2023 the New Zealand pāua export industry was estimated by Fisheries New Zealand to be worth $55 million per annum.
