# Chef references

The team's four chef portraits (`chef1` to `chef4`). `../../make_chefs.py` cuts them out of their backgrounds for the
app (the man cooks); `../../make_women_chefs.py` draws the woman cook of each region in the same style.

| Region | Man cook (team reference) | Woman cook (drawn) |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese | `chef1_asian_headband.png` | White kappogi over an indigo kimono collar, hair in a bun, tenugui headband |
| Chinese | `chef3_asian_scarf.png` | White mandarin-collar jacket with red frog buttons, hair in a bun |
| Classic (Mediterranean and the default) | `chef2_classic_white_hat.png` | White toque, double-breasted jacket, red neckerchief |
| Middle Eastern | `chef4_middle_eastern.png` | Plain hijab (white by default; black, navy and rose drawn too) with the white chef jacket and an apron |

## Sources for the clothes

- Kappogi, the Japanese cooking smock with wide sleeves, introduced at the Akahori Kappo school in 1902:
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapp%C5%8Dgi), [Kyoto Yumeyakata](https://www.en-kyoto.yumeyakata.com/kappogi-a-coat-over-kimono-for-domestic-works/),
  [Densho, a woman in a kappogi, 1910-1912](https://ddr.densho.org/ddr-densho-259-167/).
- Mandarin collar and frog buttons: [Tangzhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangzhuang),
  [Mandarin collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_collar); the white mandarin-collar samfoo top as
  women's work clothes: [Roots.sg](https://roots.gov.sg/Collection-Landing/listing/1395235).
- The Middle Eastern woman cook: no source gives a traditional kitchen outfit (home dress varies by country, and many
  women do not cover their hair at home with only family: [IslamQA](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/221744),
  [overview](https://twocontinents.com/blog/how-do-arab-women-dress)). She is drawn as a professional cook: women
  chefs who wear a hijab wear it with the white chef jacket ([Amanda Saab on MasterChef](https://mostresource.org/?p=10617)).
  Village women in southern Palestine wore plain white head veils every day until the mid-1900s
  ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/veil/jwHRZa9nqGbkYg)), the reason white is the default.
